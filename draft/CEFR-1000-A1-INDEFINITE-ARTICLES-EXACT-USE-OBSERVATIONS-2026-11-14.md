## Editorial issues and unapproved candidate improvements
- `a`: existing definition describes non-specific reference but omits **singular countable noun** and **consonant sound**, allowing mistaken teaching of `a information`, `a apples` or `a hour`. Candidate English: “An indefinite article used before a singular countable noun phrase that begins with a consonant sound, when the person or thing is not identified as a particular one.” Candidate Myanmar explanation: `တိတိကျကျ မသတ်မှတ်ထားသေးသော ရေတွက်နိုင်သည့် အနည်းကိန်းနာမ်ရှေ့တွင် ဗျည်းသံဖြင့် စလျှင် သုံးသော စကားလုံး`. Original example `I have a book.` is grammatical and can be retained.
- `an`: existing definition correctly mentions vowel **sound**, but omits singular countability and indefiniteness. Candidate English: “An indefinite article used before a singular countable noun phrase that begins with a vowel sound, when the person or thing is not identified as a particular one.” Candidate Myanmar explanation: `တိတိကျကျ မသတ်မှတ်ထားသေးသော ရေတွက်နိုင်သည့် အနည်းကိန်းနာမ်ရှေ့တွင် သရသံဖြင့် စလျှင် သုံးသော စကားလုံး`. Original `She has an apple.` is grammatical and can be retained.
- Candidate **original** contrast examples for review (not committed to learner data): “He works at a university.” / “We waited for an hour.” These test sound rather than letter; the first is /j/ initial and the second has silent h.
- Avoid equating either article directly with Burmese cardinal `တစ်` in all contexts. Burmese normally does not require an equivalent indefinite article; natural translation is contextual. Have an independent Myanmar reviewer judge clarity and whether “ရေတွက်နိုင်သည့် အနည်းကိန်းနာမ်” is too technical for A1 students.
- **Cross-entry review:** confirm that separate headwords `a` and `an` are pedagogically useful allomorph entries, not accidental semantic duplication. A strict “deduplicate senses” rule should explicitly adjudicate this pair rather than automatically discard one or silently count both as unrelated lexical senses.
- **CEFR caveat:** the British Council's A1–A2 grammar lesson is supporting curriculum context, not exact independent A1 evidence. Require an independent editor to establish appropriate entry-level evidence and approve or replace these entries.
