## Source-backed findings

1. Cambridge English Grammar Today, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/a-an-and-the (observed 2026-11-15), identifies **a/an** as indefinite articles used for a non-specific referent; **a** precedes consonant **sounds**, **an** vowel **sounds**, not necessarily consonant/vowel letters. Examples in source include *a university* and *an hour*.
2. British Council LearnEnglish, https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/indefinite-article (observed 2026-11-15), independently supports the singular-noun restriction, non-specific referent and phonological alternation; labels the grammar explanation **beginner**, not an independently validated exact-sense CEFR A1 lexeme assignment.
3. Oxford Advanced Learner's Dictionary, https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/a_1 (observed 2026-11-15), treats `an` as an alternative form of the indefinite article `a`, supporting a **same-lexeme/variant** collision risk for a manifest that requires exactly 200 deduplicated headwords/senses per level.
