## Sources observed on 2026-11-19
1. Cambridge Advanced Learner's Dictionary, **we**, GROUP sense **A1**, ALL PEOPLE sense **B1**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/we . The displayed A1 label applies to the group including the speaker; it must not be transferred to the generic ALL PEOPLE sense.
2. Cambridge English Grammar Today, personal pronouns, **We, us**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/pronouns-personal-i-me-you-him-it-they-etc . Confirms `we` is the subject form, `us` the object form; `we` may include or exclude the addressee.
3. British Council LearnEnglish, personal pronouns: https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/personal-pronouns . Beginner grammar reference lists `we` subject and `us` object; this page is **not** independent dictionary-level CEFR classification.

## Editorial findings
- **Exact sense:** provisional definition matches the Cambridge A1 GROUP sense broadly, but does not explicitly say that `we` is a **subject pronoun**. The learner must not infer that `we` is interchangeable with `us`.
- **Inclusive/exclusive ambiguity:** English `we` always includes the speaker, but may or may not include the listener. Myanmar equivalents `ကျွန်တော်တို့`, `ကျွန်မတို့`, `ကျွန်ုပ်တို့` and, in inclusive contexts, `တို့` constructions require register/context review; `ကျွန်တော်တို့` alone is not universally suitable for speakers of all genders or formalities.
- **Cross-level risk:** Cambridge separately labels the generic “all people” sense B1. Do not claim A1 coverage for this generic sense based on the group sense.
- **Original example:** “We go to school.” is grammatical as a habitual statement but provides little information about group membership. Retain it if independently judged suitable, or consider the alternatives below.
