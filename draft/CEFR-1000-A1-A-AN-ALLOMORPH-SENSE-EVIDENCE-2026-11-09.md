## Direct publisher observations (9 November 2026)
1. Cambridge Learner's Dictionary, [a](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/a): A1 determiner sense BEFORE NOUN (one non-specific person/thing), and distinct A1 ONE, EVERY/EACH, TYPE senses; A2 ACTION, TWO NOUNS, AMOUNTS senses. Cambridge groups `an` as an alternate form, not a wholly separate semantic sense. UK weak `/ə/` and strong `/eɪ/` are relevant for speech.
2. [Cambridge Grammar: A/an and the](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/the): choice depends on the *next spoken sound*, not initial written letter. `a university` (initial /j/), `an hour` (silent h). The next word can be an adjective, e.g. `an old shoe`; pronunciation must follow that word.
3. [Collins English Dictionary: a](https://www.collinsdictionary.com/dictionary/english/a): indefinite-article `a/an` first-mention/non-specific sense labelled A1; frequency/rate use `a day` is labelled A2. Collins distinguishes `A` as letter name and `a` as article.
4. [British Council: specific and general determiners](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/specific-general-determiners): general `a/an` with singular nouns, contrasted with specific determiners.
