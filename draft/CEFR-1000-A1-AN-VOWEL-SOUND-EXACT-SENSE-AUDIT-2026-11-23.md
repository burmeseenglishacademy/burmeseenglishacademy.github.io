## Evidence observed
1. Cambridge English Grammar Today, “A/an and the”, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/a-and-the (observed 2026-11-23): a/an marks an indefinite reference, and **an** precedes a vowel **sound**, not necessarily a vowel **letter**. It explicitly contrasts `an hour` with `a university` and `a one-year-old child`.
2. Same Cambridge page: a/an occurs with singular countable nouns, not bare uncountable nouns; article selection is based on the **immediately following spoken sound**, which can belong to an adjective or other modifier.
3. The grammar page does **not itself assign A1** to the exact `an` sense. A CEFR-tagged lexical source and independent human adjudication are still required; grammar support alone must not be represented as A1 validation.

## Editorial findings and proposed revisions (not applied)
- Current English definition omits indefiniteness and singular-countability. “one person or thing” is not a sufficient rule: `an honest answer` and `an interesting idea` depend on the next pronounced sound; `an information` is not normally grammatical.
- Candidate English: “The indefinite article used before a singular countable noun phrase beginning with a vowel sound, when the person or thing is not being identified as a particular known one.” Keep learner-facing wording simpler after independent review.
- Current Myanmar wording does not explicitly identify **အသံ** versus **အက္ခရာ**; “သရသံဖြင့်စသော စကားလုံး” is broadly sound-oriented, but a novice could still mistake the first written letter for the determining factor.
- Candidate learner Myanmar: “တိတိကျကျ မသတ်မှတ်ထားသေးသော လူတစ်ယောက် သို့မဟုတ် ရေတွက်နိုင်သည့် အရာတစ်ခုကို ပြောရာတွင်၊ နောက်က လိုက်လာသော စကားလုံး၏ **အသံအစ** သည် သရသံဖြစ်လျှင် သုံးသော `a` ၏ ပုံစံ။ စာလုံးအစကိုသာ မကြည့်ရပါ။” This is an editorial suggestion; a fluent independent Myanmar reviewer must validate register and clarity.
- Original candidate examples: “We waited for an hour.” — “ကျွန်တော်တို့ တစ်နာရီ စောင့်ခဲ့ကြတယ်။” / “She bought an orange umbrella.” — “သူမက လိမ္မော်ရောင် ထီးတစ်ချောင်း ဝယ်ခဲ့တယ်။”
- Original counterexamples: “He studies at a university.” (not `an university`); “I need an honest answer.” (not `a honest answer`). The initial sounds, not the spellings, determine the choice.
