## 1. need — A1 verb, necessity sense

- **Task / source index:** `CEFR1000-A1-0050` / 50.
- **Shortlist wording:** “To require something important or necessary.” Myanmar `လိုအပ်သည်`; example “I need your help.”
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the transitive necessity verb A1: if someone needs something, they must have it; it also gives the A1 `need to do` construction. It labels `don’t need to do` / `needn’t do` A2 and the “something needs doing” sense B1. The current example selects the A1 noun-complement pattern `need + noun phrase`.
- **Source:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/need
- **UK pronunciation:** /niːd/ — https://dictionary.cambridge.org/pronunciation/english/need
- **Editorial proposal:** Definition: “To have to have something, or to have to do something.” Myanmar: `တစ်စုံတစ်ရာကို မရှိမဖြစ် လိုအပ်သည်၊ သို့မဟုတ် တစ်ခုခုကို လုပ်ရန် လိုအပ်သည်။` Keep the everyday gloss `လိုအပ်သည်`.
- **Original example assessment:** “I need your help.” is grammatical and natural. Pattern: subject + present-simple transitive verb + possessive determiner + noun. It is a direct statement of need, not the A2 “you do not have to” sense.
- **New example option:** “We need more time to finish the work.” / `အလုပ်ကို ပြီးအောင်လုပ်ဖို့ ကျွန်တော်တို့ အချိန်ပိုလိုတယ်။`

## 2. come — A1 verb, movement towards the speaker/place

- **Task / source index:** `CEFR1000-A1-0058` / 58.
- **Shortlist wording:** “To move towards the speaker or a place.” Myanmar `လာသည်`; example “Please come here.”
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels A1 the movement-towards sense, the arrival sense and the “go somewhere with the speaker” sense. It identifies `come after/first/last` as B1 and the “be available in a colour/size” sense as A2 in its Essential British entry.
- **Sources:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/come ; https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/essential-british-english/come
- **UK pronunciation:** /kʌm/ — shown in both dictionary entries above.
- **Editorial proposal:** Definition: “To move or travel towards the person speaking or the place they are talking about.” Myanmar: `စကားပြောသူရှိရာသို့ သို့မဟုတ် ပြောနေသည့်နေရာသို့ လာသည်။`
- **Original example assessment:** “Please come here.” is grammatical, natural and strongly selects movement towards the speaker. Imperative `come` is followed by the place adverb `here`.
- **New example option:** “Can you come to my house after work?” / `အလုပ်ပြီးရင် ကျွန်တော့်အိမ်ကို လာနိုင်မလား။`

## 3. use — A1 verb, purpose/tool sense

- **Task / source index:** `CEFR1000-A1-0057` / 57.
- **Shortlist wording:** “To do something with a tool or object for a purpose.” Myanmar `အသုံးပြုသည်`; example “I use a pen to write.”
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Cambridge Essential British English labels A1 the verb meaning to do something with an object for a particular purpose. The same entry labels the verb sense meaning to take an amount from a supply B1, and the noun sense “purpose for which something is used” B1. The pronunciation page explicitly distinguishes verb /juːz/ from noun /juːs/.
- **Sources:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/essential-british-english/use ; https://dictionary.cambridge.org/pronunciation/english/use
- **UK pronunciation:** verb /juːz/; noun /juːs/. The shortlist is a verb, so the voiced final consonant is required.
- **Editorial proposal:** Definition: “To do something with an object, tool or skill for a particular purpose.” Myanmar: `ပစ္စည်း၊ ကိရိယာ သို့မဟုတ် ကျွမ်းကျင်မှုတစ်ခုကို ရည်ရွယ်ချက်တစ်ခုအတွက် အသုံးပြုသည်။`
- **Original example assessment:** “I use a pen to write.” is grammatical and illustrates the A1 purpose sense. Pattern: `use + object + to-infinitive`.
- **New example option:** “You can use this key to open the door.” / `ဒီသော့ကို တံခါးဖွင့်ဖို့ အသုံးပြုနိုင်တယ်။`
- **Pronunciation risk:** Do not use the noun pronunciation /juːs/ for this verb entry.

## 4. make — A1 verb, create/produce sense

- **Task / source index:** `CEFR1000-A1-0066` / 66.
- **Shortlist wording:** “To create or produce something.” Myanmar `ပြုလုပ်သည်၊ ဖန်တီးသည်`; example “We make a cake.”
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Cambridge Essential British English labels A1 “to create something”. It separately labels “be made of” and some action collocations A2, and “cause a state / force someone to do something” B1.
- **Sources:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/essential-british-english/make ; https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/make
- **UK pronunciation:** /meɪk/ — shown in the dictionary entry above.
- **Editorial proposal:** Definition: “To create or produce something.” Myanmar: `အရာဝတ္ထု သို့မဟုတ် အရာတစ်ခုကို ဖန်တီးသည်၊ ပြုလုပ်သည်။`
- **Original example assessment:** “We make a cake.” is grammatical. `make + object` selects the A1 create/produce sense. The present simple can describe a routine or general activity; add a time phrase if the intended meaning is a regular habit.
- **New example option:** “My brother makes breakfast on Sundays.” / `ကျွန်တော့်အစ်ကိုက တနင်္ဂနွေနေ့တွေမှာ မနက်စာ ပြင်ဆင်တယ်။`
- **Cross-level warning:** Do not use this A1 label to justify `make someone happy`, `make someone do something`, or every collocation with `make`; the inspected entry labels important related senses B1 or gives them separately.

## 5. look — A1 verb, direct visual-attention sense

- **Task / source index:** `CEFR1000-A1-0067` / 67.
- **Shortlist wording:** “To direct your eyes towards something.” Myanmar `ကြည့်သည်`; example “Look at the board.”
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels A1 the “turn your eyes in the direction” sense and the “try to find” sense. It labels appearance `look nice/strange` A2 and probability `it looks like/as if` B1. Cambridge grammar explicitly gives `look at + object` and warns against omitting `at` in that construction.
- **Sources:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/look ; https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/look
- **UK pronunciation:** /lʊk/ — shown in the Learner’s Dictionary entry.
- **Editorial proposal:** Definition: “To turn your eyes towards someone or something so that you can see them or it.” Myanmar: `တစ်စုံတစ်ယောက် သို့မဟုတ် အရာတစ်ခုကို မြင်နိုင်ရန် မျက်လုံးကို ထိုဘက်သို့ လှည့်ကြည့်သည်။`
- **Original example assessment:** “Look at the board.” is a correct imperative with the required preposition `at`. It selects the visual-attention sense, not “appear”.
- **New example option:** “Look at the map before we leave.” / `မထွက်ခွာခင် မြေပုံကို ကြည့်ပါ။`
- **Grammar warning:** Teach `look at the picture`, not `look the picture`. Distinguish `look at` (direct visual attention) from `see` (perceive with the eyes).

## 6. take — A1 verb, carry-with-you sense

- **Task / source index:** `CEFR1000-A1-0068` / 68.
- **Shortlist wording:** “To pick up, carry or move something.” Myanmar `ယူသည်`; example “Please take this chair.”
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Cambridge Essential British English labels A1 “get and carry something with you when you go somewhere” and the sense of taking someone somewhere. It labels travelling by bus/train/car and choosing a road/turning A2.
- **Source:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/essential-british-english/take
- **UK pronunciation:** /teɪk/; irregular forms: `take–took–taken` (shown in the source).
- **Editorial proposal:** The current definition is too broad because it can imply merely lifting or moving an object. Prefer: “To get something and carry it with you when you go somewhere.” Myanmar: `တစ်နေရာရာသို့ သွားသည့်အခါ ပစ္စည်းတစ်ခုကို ယူဆောင်သွားသည်။`
- **Original example assessment:** “Please take this chair.” is grammatical but does not make the “carry with you when you go somewhere” feature explicit. It can be read as a general instruction to move the chair.
- **New example option:** “Please take your umbrella with you.” / `ထီးကိုလည်း ယူသွားပါ။`
- **Cross-level warning:** Do not transfer the A1 carry-with-you label to `take the bus` or `take the third turning`; the inspected Essential British entry labels those A2.

## 7. give — A1 verb, provide-someone-with-something sense; current example mismatch

- **Task / source index:** `CEFR1000-A1-0064` / 64.
- **Shortlist wording:** “To hand something to someone.” Myanmar `ပေးသည်`; example “Please give me the book.”
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels A1 “provide someone with something”. It labels “put something near someone or in their hand” A2, as well as the message sense A2. Thus the current definition and example select the A2 hand-transfer sense, not the A1 provide sense. This is a material definition/example/level mismatch.
- **Source:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/give
- **UK pronunciation:** /ɡɪv/ — shown in the Learner’s Dictionary entry.
- **Editorial proposal:** For A1, change the definition to “To provide someone with something.” Myanmar: `တစ်စုံတစ်ယောက်အား တစ်စုံတစ်ရာကို ပေးအပ်သည်၊ ဖြည့်ဆည်းပေးသည်။` The simple gloss `ပေးသည်` is acceptable but does not by itself distinguish provision from physical hand-transfer.
- **Current example disposition:** “Please give me the book.” is grammatical, but it most directly illustrates the A2 put-in-someone’s-hand sense in the inspected source. Do not approve it as the A1 example without a reviewer explicitly defending a different sense analysis.
- **New A1 example option:** “The school gives every child a free book.” / `ကျောင်းက ကလေးတိုင်းကို အခမဲ့ စာအုပ်တစ်အုပ်စီ ပေးတယ်။` This foregrounds provision and does not require the physical transfer to occur at the moment of speaking.
- **Required human decision:** The independent reviewer should decide whether to retain A1 with the provision sense and replace the example, or reallocate the hand-transfer sense to A2. Do not change the shortlist level or task status in this evidence-only step.

## 8. old — A1 adjective, age/existence-duration sense

- **Task / source index:** `CEFR1000-A1-0060` / 60.
- **Shortlist wording:** “Having lived or existed for many years.” Myanmar `အသက်ကြီးသော၊ ဟောင်းသော`; example “My grandfather is old.”
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Cambridge Essential British English labels A1 the “lived or existed for a long time” sense, the “used or owned for a long time” sense, and `How old are you?` / `years old`. It labels “used before/in the past” and the “old friend” sense A2.
- **Source:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/essential-british-english/old
- **UK pronunciation:** /əʊld/ — shown in the source.
- **Editorial proposal:** For the selected human-age example, define: “Having lived for a long time.” Myanmar: `အသက်ရှင်နေထိုင်ခဲ့သည့် ကာလရှည်ကြာသော၊ အသက်ကြီးသော။` Reserve `ဟောင်းသော` for objects that have been used or owned for a long time; do not merge the human-age and object-age nuances into one undifferentiated Burmese gloss.
- **Original example assessment:** “My grandfather is old.” is grammatical and selects the human-age sense. It is understandable, though age descriptions can be context-sensitive; an age-number example may be more neutral for a beginner.
- **New example option:** “My grandfather is eighty years old.” / `ကျွန်တော့်အဘိုးက အသက်ရှစ်ဆယ်ရှိပြီ။`
