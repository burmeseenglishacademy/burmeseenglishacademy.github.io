## we — pronoun, A1 speaker-plus-others sense
- Cambridge Learner's Dictionary, observed 2026-11-06: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/we — specifically marks first-person plural subject sense A1; generic people sense B1.
- Cambridge English Dictionary, observed 2026-11-06: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/we — GROUP sense A1; ALL PEOPLE sense B1; also records informal 'you' and royal 'I' senses that must not be silently merged into A1.
- Collins COBUILD, observed 2026-11-06: https://www.collinsdictionary.com/dictionary/english/we — speaker plus others A1; people in general B1.

Candidate original learner-facing English: 'The subject pronoun for the speaker together with one or more other people.' Candidate Myanmar: 'စကားပြောသူကိုယ်တိုင်နှင့် အခြားလူတစ်ဦး သို့မဟုတ် တစ်ဦးထက်ပိုသူများကို ပေါင်း၍ ရည်ညွှန်းသော နာမ်စား။' Candidate original example: 'We walk to school together.' Candidate Myanmar example: 'ကျွန်တော်တို့ ကျောင်းကို အတူတူ လမ်းလျှောက်သွားကြတယ်။'

Reviewer questions: Confirm natural Myanmar register and whether 'ကျွန်မတို့' should also be shown; note inclusive/exclusive listener context; ensure subject form `we` is not confused with object `us`. Reject an A1 example using the generic B1 sense. Current shortlist example 'We go to school.' is grammatically viable but requires independent review.

## the — definite article, A1 identifiable-referent sense
- Collins COBUILD, observed 2026-11-06: https://www.collinsdictionary.com/dictionary/english/the — determiner A1 for an already mentioned or identified referent; multiple separate A1 uses require exact-sense care.
- Cambridge English Grammar Today, observed 2026-11-06: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/a-and-the — contrasts the identifiable definite article with indefinite a/an; grammar corroboration, NOT a standalone CEFR level assertion.
- Cambridge English Grammar Today, observed 2026-11-06: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/determiners-the-my-some-this — confirms articles as determiners and the need to distinguish specific from generic reference; NOT CEFR grading evidence.

Candidate original learner-facing English: 'Used before a noun when the listener or reader can identify the particular person or thing meant.' Candidate Myanmar: 'ပြောသူနှင့် နားထောင်သူတို့ သိထားပြီးသား သို့မဟုတ် မည်သည့်အရာဖြစ်ကြောင်း သတ်မှတ်နိုင်သော နာမ်ရှေ့တွင် သုံးသည့် အညွှန်းစကားလုံး။' Candidate original context-and-example: 'I bought a notebook yesterday. The notebook is blue.' Candidate Myanmar: 'မနေ့က မှတ်စုစာအုပ်တစ်အုပ် ဝယ်ခဲ့တယ်။ အဲဒီမှတ်စုစာအုပ်က အပြာရောင်ပါ။'

Reviewer questions: Confirm context-dependent definiteness, distinguish from a/an, avoid claiming Burmese has a directly equivalent obligatory article, and check pronunciation variation /ðə/ versus /ði/ before sound contexts. The shortlist's 'The book is on the table.' is grammatical but does not independently establish whether both nouns are identifiable in context.
