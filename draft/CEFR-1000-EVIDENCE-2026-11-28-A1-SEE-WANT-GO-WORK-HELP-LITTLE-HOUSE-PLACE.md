## 1. see — A1 verb, use-the-eyes sense

- **Task / source index:** `CEFR1000-A1-0046` / 45.
- **Shortlist wording:** “To notice something with your eyes.” Myanmar `မြင်သည်၊ တွေ့သည်`; example “I can see the moon.”
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the verb sense “notice people and things with your eyes” A1. The same entry separately labels “understand” B1 and watching a film or programme A2; meeting or visiting someone is also separately listed at A1. Cambridge grammar explains that `see` means noticing something using the eyes and distinguishes it from `look at` (directing the eyes) and `watch` (looking over a period, especially at something moving or changing).
- **Sources:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/see ; https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/see-look-at-or-watch
- **UK pronunciation:** /siː/ — shown in the Learner’s Dictionary entry.
- **Editorial proposal:** Keep the selected definition. For this visual sense, prefer Myanmar `မျက်စိဖြင့် မြင်သည်၊ တွေ့မြင်သည်။` The shorter gloss `တွေ့သည်` can also suggest the separately listed meeting sense, so it should not stand alone if exact-sense distinction is required.
- **Original example assessment:** “I can see the moon.” is grammatical and natural. The modal `can` is idiomatic when describing what is visible; the moon is the object of visual perception.
- **Alternative example:** “I can see the sea from my window.” / `ကျွန်တော့်ပြတင်းပေါက်ကနေ ပင်လယ်ကို မြင်ရတယ်။`
- **Cross-level warning:** Do not use this A1 label to justify the B1 “understand” sense or the A2 “watch a programme” sense.

## 2. want — A1 verb, wish/desire sense

- **Task / source index:** `CEFR1000-A1-0048` / 47.
- **Shortlist wording:** “To wish to have or do something.” Myanmar `လိုချင်သည်၊ ဆန္ဒရှိသည်`; example “I want some water.”
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Cambridge English Dictionary labels the verb sense “wish for a particular thing or plan of action” A1. Cambridge grammar confirms that this use takes a complement, commonly a noun/pronoun object or a `to`-infinitive. The grammar entry separately discusses informal British `want` meaning “need” and advice uses, which should not be folded into this beginner sense.
- **Sources:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/want ; https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/want
- **UK pronunciation:** /wɒnt/ — shown in the Cambridge English Dictionary entry.
- **Editorial proposal:** Keep the core meaning. A more directly learner-facing Myanmar rendering is `တစ်ခုခုကို လိုချင်သည်၊ တစ်ခုခုကို လုပ်ချင်သည်။` `ဆန္ဒရှိသည်` is understandable but more formal and can be less direct in an everyday beginner example.
- **Original example assessment:** “I want some water.” is grammatical and natural. `some water` is the noun-phrase complement and clearly selects the wish/desire sense.
- **Alternative example:** “I want to learn English.” / `အင်္ဂလိပ်စာ သင်ယူချင်တယ်။`
- **Cross-level warning:** Do not treat every British `want` construction as the A1 desire sense. The informal `want + -ing` meaning “need doing” and advice uses require separate treatment.

## 3. go — A1 verb, movement/travel sense

- **Task / source index:** `CEFR1000-A1-0049` / 48.
- **Shortlist wording:** “To move or travel from one place to another.” Myanmar `သွားသည်`; example “We go to the park.”
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the intransitive MOVE sense A1 and also labels moving/travelling somewhere in order to do something A1. Cambridge grammar notes that `go` normally refers to movement away from the speaker/listener and is normally used without a direct object; place information is supplied by an adverb or prepositional phrase.
- **Sources:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/go ; https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/go
- **UK pronunciation:** /ɡəʊ/ — shown in the Learner’s Dictionary entry.
- **Editorial proposal:** The definition and Myanmar gloss `သွားသည်` are suitable for this intransitive movement sense.
- **Original example assessment:** “We go to the park.” is grammatical: `go` is intransitive and `to the park` supplies the destination. It is natural as a habitual statement, although a time phrase could make the habitual reading more explicit.
- **Alternative example:** “I go to work by bus.” / `ကျွန်တော် အလုပ်ကို ဘတ်စ်ကားနဲ့ သွားတယ်။`
- **Grammar warning:** Do not teach `go` as taking a direct object in this sense; distinguish `go` from `walk`, and teach `go + -ing` as a separate activity pattern where appropriate.

## 4. work — A1 verb, doing a job

- **Task / source index:** `CEFR1000-A1-0053` / 52.
- **Shortlist wording:** “To do a job or activity that needs effort.” Myanmar `အလုပ်လုပ်သည်`; example “I work in a shop.”
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the verb JOB sense A1: doing a job, especially one done to earn money. It labels the machine/equipment sense A2. In the noun entry, “use of physical or mental effort” is B1, while the job and workplace noun senses are A1.
- **Source:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/work
- **UK pronunciation:** /wɜːk/ — shown in the Learner’s Dictionary entry.
- **Editorial proposal:** Narrow the English definition to “To do a job, especially one you do to earn money.” The current phrase “or activity that needs effort” is too broad and risks blending the A1 job verb with the B1 effort noun. Myanmar `အလုပ်အကိုင်တစ်ခုကို လုပ်ကိုင်သည်။` is a suitable fuller gloss; `အလုပ်လုပ်သည်` remains a clear short gloss.
- **Original example assessment:** “I work in a shop.” is grammatical and naturally selects the A1 job sense. The prepositional phrase `in a shop` gives the workplace.
- **Alternative example:** “My sister works at a bank.” / `ကျွန်တော့်ညီမက ဘဏ်မှာ အလုပ်လုပ်တယ်။`
- **Cross-level warning:** Keep “the work took a lot of effort” (noun) and “the machine works” (verb) separate from this A1 job sense.

## 5. help — A1 verb, assisting a person

- **Task / source index:** `CEFR1000-A1-0059` / 58.
- **Shortlist wording:** “To make a task easier for someone.” Myanmar `ကူညီသည်`; example “Can you help me?”
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels `help` verb (GIVE HELP) A1 and gives the pattern `help someone (to) do something`. It separately labels the sense “make something easier or better” B2. The current entry is the person-assistance sense, not the separate inanimate-subject construction.
- **Source:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/help
- **UK pronunciation:** /help/ — shown in the Learner’s Dictionary entry.
- **Editorial proposal:** Refine the definition to “To make it easier for someone to do something.” Myanmar `တစ်စုံတစ်ဦးကို တစ်ခုခုလုပ်ရာတွင် ကူညီသည်။` retains the person being helped and the activity.
- **Original example assessment:** “Can you help me?” is grammatical and idiomatic. It is a complete, natural request, although it leaves the activity unstated.
- **Alternative example:** “Can you help me with my homework?” / `အိမ်စာလုပ်ရာမှာ ကူညီပေးနိုင်မလား။`
- **Cross-level warning:** Do not use the A1 person-assistance label as evidence that every `help + thing` construction or every “make something easier” sense is A1.

## 6. little — A1 adjective, small-size sense

- **Task / source index:** `CEFR1000-A1-0061` / 60.
- **Shortlist wording:** “Small in size or amount.” Myanmar `သေးငယ်သော၊ နည်းသော`; example “I have a little dog.”
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the adjective SMALL sense A1 and also lists the adjective meaning “young” at A1. It labels the quantifier meaning “not much or not enough” B1 and the adverb use “slightly” A2. The shortlist explicitly marks this item as an adjective.
- **Source:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/little
- **UK pronunciation:** /ˈlɪtl/ — shown in the Learner’s Dictionary entry.
- **Editorial proposal:** For the adjective entry, prefer Myanmar `သေးငယ်သော၊ အသေးစားဖြစ်သော။` The gloss `နည်းသော` can lead learners towards the separate B1 quantifier sense and should not be presented as an interchangeable adjective translation without context.
- **Original example assessment:** “I have a little dog.” is grammatical and natural. It can mean a dog that is small or young; both readings are listed as A1 adjective senses, so it does not uniquely isolate size.
- **Alternative example:** “The little box is on the table.” / `သေတ္တာအသေးလေးက စားပွဲပေါ်မှာ ရှိတယ်။`
- **Cross-level warning:** Keep adjective `little` separate from B1 quantifier `little` (“not much/not enough”) and A2 adverb `a little` (“slightly”).

## 7. house — A1 noun, residential building

- **Task / source index:** `CEFR1000-A1-0062` / 61.
- **Shortlist wording:** “A building in which people live.” Myanmar `အိမ်`; example “Our house has two rooms.”
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the count-noun BUILDING sense A1: a building where people live, usually one family or group. The entry separately lists a collective sense referring to the people who live in a house and other institutional/activity-place senses.
- **Source:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/house
- **UK pronunciation:** /haʊs/ (noun); the entry also shows the plural `houses` /ˈhaʊzɪz/.
- **Editorial proposal:** The English definition and Myanmar gloss `အိမ်` are accurate for the selected residential-building sense.
- **Original example assessment:** “Our house has two rooms.” is grammatical and clearly selects the building sense. `house` is a count noun; the possessive determiner `our` is correctly used.
- **Alternative example:** “Their house is near the school.” / `သူတို့အိမ်က ကျောင်းနားမှာ ရှိတယ်။`
- **Grammar note:** Preserve the count-noun distinction: `a house / two houses`; do not confuse `house` with uncountable `housing`.

## 8. place — A1 noun, somewhere/location sense

- **Task / source index:** `CEFR1000-A1-0063` / 62.
- **Shortlist wording:** “A particular location or area.” Myanmar `နေရာ`; example “This is a quiet place.”
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the noun SOMEWHERE sense A1: a position, building, town or area. The noun HOME sense is A2; OPPORTUNITY and numbered position senses are B1; the phrase `take place` meaning “happen” is B1. The verb `place` meaning to put something somewhere carefully is B2.
- **Source:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/place
- **UK pronunciation:** /pleɪs/ — shown in the Learner’s Dictionary entry.
- **Editorial proposal:** Keep the definition and Myanmar gloss `နေရာ`; they fit the A1 noun SOMEWHERE sense.
- **Original example assessment:** “This is a quiet place.” is grammatical and natural. The determiner `a` correctly introduces the singular count noun; `quiet` is an adjective modifier.
- **Alternative example:** “This is a good place to study.” / `ဒီနေရာက စာလေ့လာဖို့ ကောင်းတယ်။`
- **Cross-level warning:** Do not infer the CEFR level of the A2 home sense, B1 opportunity sense or B2 verb from the A1 noun entry.

## Cross-entry findings and reviewer handoff

1. **Definition narrowing needed:** `work` should be narrowed to the A1 paid-job verb. The current “activity that needs effort” wording risks importing the B1 noun sense.
2. **Myanmar sense separation needed:** For adjective `little`, `သေးငယ်သော` is the safer primary gloss. `နည်းသော` risks conflating the A1 adjective with the B1 quantifier.
3. **Visual-sense clarity:** For `see`, a Myanmar gloss that explicitly mentions seeing with the eyes is clearer than `တွေ့သည်` alone, which can also mean meeting someone.
4. **Pattern examples:** The existing `go`, `work`, `house` and `place` examples are grammatical and select their intended A1 senses. The `help` example is natural; a complement can make the assisted activity more explicit. The `want` example correctly uses a noun complement.
5. **No CEFR propagation:** A headword's A1 label does not assign A1 to all of its senses, parts of speech, collocations or constructions. The cross-level distinctions above must remain visible to independent reviewers.
6. **No status changes:** This note does not edit the shortlist or review-task JSON. All eight tasks remain pending; all entries remain on editorial hold. No `reviewed_*`, reviewer identity, signature, evidence approval or release flag is set.
