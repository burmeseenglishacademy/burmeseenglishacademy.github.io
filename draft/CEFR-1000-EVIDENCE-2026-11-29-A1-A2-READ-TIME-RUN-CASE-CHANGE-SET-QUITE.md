## 1. read — A1 verb, understand written words

- **Task:** `CEFR1000-A1-0031`; source index 30.
- **Shortlist:** “To look at written words and understand them.” Myanmar `ဖတ်သည်`; example “I read a book every night.”
- **Exact-sense / CEFR evidence:** Cambridge English Dictionary marks the verb sense “look at words or symbols and understand what they mean” A1 [I or T]. The selected example uses the present-simple base form for a habitual action and clearly selects this reading sense.
- **Sources:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/read ; UK pronunciation and homograph contrast: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/pronunciation/english/read
- **UK pronunciation risk:** The headword/base-form verb is /riːd/. The past-tense form is spelled identically but pronounced /red/. The example “I read a book every night” is present simple and therefore uses /riːd/, not /red/. The headword audio must not be generated from a past-tense interpretation.
- **Editorial assessment:** English definition, Myanmar gloss and example align with the A1 sense. `ဖတ်သည်` is concise and accurate. Keep the base-form pronunciation distinction visible to the audio listener.
- **Proposed action:** Retain the sense and example provisionally; require the independent reviewer and final audio listener to confirm the intended present/base-form reading. No shortlist change.

## 2. time — A1 noun, a particular point in the day

- **Task:** `CEFR1000-A1-0042`; source index 42.
- **Shortlist:** “What clocks measure in hours and minutes.” Myanmar `အချိန်`; example “What time is it?”
- **Exact-sense / CEFR evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary marks “a particular point in the day or night” A1 and gives “What time is it?” as its example. The same entry marks the broader measurable-duration sense (“what we measure in minutes, hours, days, etc.”) A2.
- **Source:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/time
- **Mismatch:** The example selects the A1 clock-time/particular-point sense, but the current English definition describes the broader A2 duration sense. The Burmese gloss `အချိန်` is valid but broad enough to cover both.
- **Editorial proposal:** Definition: “A particular point in the day or night, usually shown on a clock.” Myanmar support gloss: `နေ့ သို့မဟုတ် ညအတွင်း နာရီဖြင့် ဖော်ပြသော အချိန်`. Keep “What time is it?” as the example.
- **Cross-level warning:** Do not use the A1 clock-time evidence to justify the A2 duration, period, occasion or “have time” senses. Those are separately labelled in the Cambridge entry.
- **Proposed action:** Retain A1; narrow the definition to the exact example sense, subject to independent bilingual review.

## 3. run — A1 verb, move faster than walking

- **Task:** `CEFR1000-A1-0195`; source index 196.
- **Shortlist:** “To move on foot faster than walking.” Myanmar `ပြေးသည်`; example “I run in the park.”
- **Exact-sense / CEFR evidence:** Cambridge Essential British English marks this movement verb A1 and defines it as moving on one’s feet faster than walking. It separately labels the organisation/control sense B1; its noun sense for a sporting run is B1.
- **Source:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/essential-british-english/run
- **UK pronunciation / forms:** /rʌn/; `run–ran–run`.
- **Editorial assessment:** The definition, Myanmar gloss and example align with the A1 movement sense. The present simple is natural for a habit or routine. The example does not select the B1 business-management or operating-equipment senses.
- **Proposed action:** Retain provisionally; keep the verb entry distinct from the B1 noun and other verb senses.

## 4. case — A2 noun, current “situation/example” sense is B1

- **Task:** `CEFR1000-A2-0215`; source index 190.
- **Shortlist:** “A particular situation or example of something.” Myanmar `အခြေအနေ၊ ဖြစ်ရပ်`; example “In this case, we should wait.”
- **Exact-sense / CEFR evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary marks the selected “particular situation or example” noun sense B1. It marks the container sense A2 (a container for storing or protecting something) and the suitcase/bag sense A2.
- **Source:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/case
- **Mismatch:** The current English definition, Myanmar meaning and example consistently select the B1 situation/example sense, not an A2 sense. This is a direct shortlist-level conflict, not merely a broad-definition concern.
- **A2 alternative if the A2 slot is retained:** Definition: “A container used to keep or protect something.” Myanmar proposal: `ပစ္စည်းတစ်ခုကို သိမ်းဆည်းရန် သို့မဟုတ် ကာကွယ်ရန် အသုံးပြုသော ဘူး သို့မဟုတ် အိတ်`. Example proposal: “I keep my glasses in a case.” / `မျက်မှန်ကို ဘူးထဲမှာ သိမ်းထားတယ်။`
- **Alternative editorial decision:** If the situation/example sense is the intended learning target, it should be considered for B1 with a controlled cross-level replacement so that each level remains at exactly 200 entries. Do not silently move it or change level counts.
- **Proposed action:** Keep the current entry on hold. Independent reviewers must choose and document one exact sense; do not approve the current A2 wording as-is.

## 5. change — A2 verb, “become/make different” versus A1 exchange

- **Task:** `CEFR1000-A2-0217`; source index 192.
- **Shortlist:** “To make or become different.” Myanmar `ပြောင်းလဲသည်`; example “We need to change the meeting time.”
- **Exact-sense / CEFR evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary separates A2 “to become different, or to make someone or something become different” from A1 “to stop having or using one thing, and start having or using another.” Its A1 examples include changing a scheduled interview time; its A2 examples show a person, company, landscape or situation becoming different.
- **Sources:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/change ; https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/essential-british-english/change
- **Mismatch risk:** The definition is A2, but “change the meeting time” can naturally be understood as replacing one scheduled time with another, selecting the A1 exchange sense. The example therefore does not securely disambiguate the A2 sense.
- **A2 example proposal:** “The weather can change quickly.” / `ရာသီဥတုက အလျင်အမြန် ပြောင်းလဲနိုင်တယ်။` This selects becoming different without an exchange object.
- **Editorial proposal:** Retain the A2 definition and Myanmar core gloss `ပြောင်းလဲသည်`; replace the example only after independent review. Keep A1 `change one thing for another` separate (for example, change jobs or change the time of an appointment).
- **Proposed action:** Do not reclassify the current A2 item or alter the shortlist yet. Record the example-sense ambiguity for independent review.

## 6. set — A2 verb, place/position sense; CEFR label not established

- **Task:** `CEFR1000-A2-0278`; source index 505.
- **Shortlist:** “To put something in a particular place or position.” Myanmar `တစ်နေရာတွင် ထားသည်၊ ချထားသည်`; example “Please set the clean cups on the table.”
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Oxford Advanced Learner’s Dictionary directly defines the selected construction `set something + adverb/preposition` as putting something in a particular place or position, with “She set a tray down on the table.” as an example. Cambridge Essential British English labels several other verb senses B1 (arrange a time, prepare equipment, sun going down, story location) and the noun “a group of things that belong together” A2, but does not assign an explicit CEFR label to the selected placement verb sense.
- **Sources:** https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/set_1 ; https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/essential-british-english/set
- **Assessment:** The exact verb sense and the shortlist example align. The Myanmar wording is broadly accurate, though `ထားသည်၊ ချထားသည်` can overlap with the more general verb “put” and does not by itself convey the positioning nuance.
- **CEFR limitation:** The inspected Oxford sense page provides no A2 label for this sense. The Cambridge A2 label belongs to the noun “set”, not this verb; its B1 labels belong to other verb senses. Neither should be transferred to this entry. The A2 assignment is therefore not yet source-substantiated.
- **Myanmar refinement proposal:** `အရာတစ်ခုကို သတ်မှတ်ထားသော နေရာ သို့မဟုတ် အနေအထားတွင် ထားသည်။`
- **Proposed action:** Keep on hold pending an authoritative exact-sense CEFR source or a documented independent decision that explains the level evidence. Do not infer A2 from the word’s other senses.

## 7. quite — A2 adverb, moderate degree with gradable adjectives

- **Task:** `CEFR1000-A2-0340`; source index 751.
- **Shortlist:** “To a fairly large degree, but not completely.” Myanmar `အတော်အတန်`; example “The journey was quite long.”
- **Exact-sense / CEFR evidence:** Cambridge Essential British English marks the “a little or a lot but not completely” adverb A2 (UK) and marks the “completely” sense B1. Cambridge’s learner entry also distinguishes A2 incomplete/moderate degree from B1 “completely”.
- **Sources:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/essential-british-english/quite ; https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/quite
- **Editorial assessment:** `long` is a gradable adjective, so “quite long” selects the A2 moderate-degree reading rather than the B1 complete reading. Myanmar `အတော်အတန်` is a reasonable concise gloss; avoid teaching it as always meaning “very” or “completely”.
- **Proposed action:** Retain provisionally. Independent review should confirm the British-English pragmatic nuance and whether the Myanmar gloss is sufficiently clear for beginners.
