## Cross-sense and grammar risks for independent adjudication
- **A2 requirement:** “You must wear a helmet.” = safety obligation.
- **B1 suggestion:** “You must visit us sometime.” = enthusiastic recommendation, not a legal duty.
- **B2 inference:** “She must be tired after that journey.” = confident deduction, not an instruction.
- **Negation:** `mustn't` generally means prohibition; `don't have to` means lack of necessity. They are **not** interchangeable. The example “You mustn't wear a helmet” reverses the intended safety message, while “You don't have to wear a helmet” removes the obligation.
- **Form:** `must` is followed by the base verb (`must wear`, not `must to wear`); it does not take `-s` for third-person singular.
- Avoid copying Cambridge's example sentences into the curriculum. All candidate examples above are original and require a separate grammar/meaning check.
