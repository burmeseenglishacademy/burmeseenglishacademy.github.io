## water — A1 noun, liquid sense — shortlist index 20

Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary explicitly labels the liquid noun sense A1: “the clear liquid that falls from the sky as rain and that is in seas, lakes, and rivers.” The learner entry separately labels the sea/river/lake area sense A2. It also lists the verb “water” at B2 for pouring water over plants. Keep the shortlist’s noun/liquid sense distinct from those other senses and the verb.

- Source: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/water
- British pronunciation shown by Cambridge: /ˈwɔːtə(r)/.
- Proposed concise English definition: “the clear liquid that people drink.”
- Proposed Myanmar meaning: “ရေ (သောက်သုံးသော အရည်)”.
- Existing shortlist example: “I drink water every day.” This is grammatical and sense-aligned: simple present, uncountable liquid noun without an article, and a frequency phrase.
- Proposed Myanmar example: “ကျွန်တော်/ကျွန်မ နေ့တိုင်း ရေသောက်တယ်။”
- Editorial note: do not confuse the liquid sense with plural “waters” meaning areas of sea/river, or the B2 plant-watering verb.

## food — A1 noun, nourishment sense — shortlist index 21

Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the noun A1 and defines it as something people and animals eat, or plants absorb, to keep them alive. The entry marks the noun as both countable and uncountable; the everyday general-food use in the shortlist example is uncountable.

- Source: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/food
- British pronunciation shown by Cambridge: /fuːd/.
- Proposed concise English definition: “something that people or animals eat to stay alive.”
- Proposed Myanmar meaning: “အစားအစာ၊ စားစရာ”.
- Existing shortlist example: “The food is ready.” This is grammatical; “food” is used as an uncountable noun referring to the food prepared.
- Proposed Myanmar example: “စားစရာ အဆင်သင့်ဖြစ်ပြီ။”
- Editorial note: retain the A1 nourishment sense. Do not imply that every use is uncountable; types or servings can use count syntax in suitable contexts.

## family — A1 noun, related-people sense — shortlist index 23

Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the related-people noun sense A1: a group of people related to each other, such as a mother, father and children. The same entry separately labels “the children in a family” B1. It also gives a plant/animal grouping sense without an explicit CEFR label in the retrieved entry; do not assign that sense a level from this evidence.

- Source: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/family
- British pronunciation shown by Cambridge: /ˈfæm.ə.li/ (syllabification may vary by transcription display).
- Proposed concise English definition: “a group of people who are related to each other, such as parents and their children.”
- Proposed Myanmar meaning: “မိသားစု၊ ဆွေမျိုးသားချင်းများ”.
- Existing shortlist example: “My family lives here.” This is grammatical with the collective noun treated as one unit and singular verb agreement. British English can also use plural agreement when emphasising the individual members; do not present one pattern as the only possible British usage.
- Proposed Myanmar example: “ကျွန်တော့်/ကျွန်မ မိသားစုက ဒီမှာ နေတယ်။”
- Editorial note: the B1 “children in a family” sense is not the selected A1 sense. Preserve the exact-sense distinction.

## friend — A1 noun, familiar-person sense — shortlist index 76

Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the noun A1: someone you know well and like. The entry separately labels “an old friend” A2 and the phrases “be friends (with someone)” and “make friends (with someone)” B1. Those phrase/extended senses must not be used to relabel the basic A1 noun.

- Source: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/friend
- British pronunciation shown by Cambridge: /frend/.
- Proposed concise English definition: “someone you know well and like.”
- Proposed Myanmar meaning: “သူငယ်ချင်း၊ မိတ်ဆွေ”.
- Existing shortlist example: “My friend lives nearby.” This is grammatical and sense-aligned; it uses a singular count noun with a possessive determiner and third-person singular present-tense verb.
- Proposed Myanmar example: “ကျွန်တော့်/ကျွန်မ သူငယ်ချင်းက အနီးအနားမှာ နေတယ်။”
- Editorial note: A2 “old friend” means someone known and liked for a long time; B1 “be/make friends” are phrase-level uses, not evidence that the A1 noun itself is B1.
