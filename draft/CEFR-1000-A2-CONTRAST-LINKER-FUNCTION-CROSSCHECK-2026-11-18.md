## Exact-sense and grammar findings
**though**: The provisional example `Though the shop was busy, the staff were helpful.` correctly uses a subordinating concessive conjunction, followed by a full subordinate clause and comma before the main clause. Cambridge separately documents sentence-final **though** as a spoken adverb similar to **however**. These are distinct grammatical functions: the shortlist must not silently absorb the sentence-final sense into the conjunction entry. Proposed tighter English definition for *conjunction only*: “Introduces a clause describing a fact that does not prevent the contrasting fact in the main clause.” Proposed natural Myanmar teaching meaning: “...ပေမယ့် / ...သော်လည်း (မျှော်လင့်ထားသလို မဖြစ်ဘဲ ဆန့်ကျင်သည့်အချက်ကို ဆက်ပြောရာတွင် သုံးသည်)”. Original example: “Though the bus was late, we reached the station on time.” Myanmar rendering: “ဘတ်စ်ကား နောက်ကျခဲ့ပေမယ့် ဘူတာကို အချိန်မီ ရောက်ခဲ့တယ်။”

**however**: The provisional `The room was small; however, it was comfortable.` is a defensible two-independent-clause construction with a semicolon and comma; it is **not** a subordinating conjunction interchangeable with **though**. Cambridge explicitly warns against using **however** as a conjunction in place of **but**. Proposed tighter English definition: “A linking adverb used to introduce a sentence or independent clause that contrasts with a previous statement.” Proposed Myanmar explanation: “သို့သော် / ဒါပေမဲ့ (ရှေ့က ပြောထားသည့်အချက်နှင့် ဆန့်ကျင်သည့် အကြောင်းကို ဝါကျအသစ်ဖြင့် ဆက်ပြောရာတွင် သုံးသည်)”. Original example: “The lesson was difficult. However, the teacher explained it clearly.” Myanmar rendering: “သင်ခန်းစာက ခက်ခဲတယ်။ ဒါပေမဲ့ ဆရာက ရှင်းရှင်းလင်းလင်း ရှင်းပြပေးခဲ့တယ်။”

**Contrast trap:** `Though it rained, we played.` is a valid subordinate-main clause pattern; `It rained; however, we played.` is a valid independent-clause pattern. `However it rained, we played` is **not** a grammatical substitution for the intended concessive construction. Avoid teaching `however` as an ordinary coordinator or allowing a comma splice.
