## Findings

### 1. book — noun

- **Task / origin:** `CEFR1000-A1-0030`; source index 29.
- **Shortlist definition and meaning:** “Pages with words or pictures bound together.” / `စာအုပ်`.
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Cambridge labels the noun “book” A1 [C] for a written text and separately A1 [C] for a set of pages fastened together inside a cover to be read or written in. The second sense is the closest match to the shortlist definition. UK pronunciation shown: `/bʊk/`.
- **Definition refinement:** “A set of pages fastened together inside a cover, for reading or writing.”
- **Myanmar refinement:** `အဖုံးတစ်ခုအတွင်း စုစည်းချုပ်ထားပြီး ဖတ်ရှုရန် သို့မဟုတ် ရေးသားရန် အသုံးပြုသော စာမျက်နှာများပါဝင်သည့် စာအုပ်။`
- **Original example proposal:** “I borrowed a book from the library.”
- **Myanmar example:** `ကျွန်တော် စာကြည့်တိုက်ကနေ စာအုပ်တစ်အုပ် ငှားယူခဲ့တယ်။`
- **Grammar/editorial note:** Countable noun; singular count use requires a determiner (“a book”, “the book”, “my book”). The shortlist example “This book is interesting” is grammatical and natural.

Sources:
- Cambridge dictionary exact noun senses and A1 labels: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/book

### 2. chair — noun

- **Task / origin:** `CEFR1000-A1-0095`; source index 95.
- **Shortlist definition and meaning:** “A piece of furniture for one person to sit on.” / `ထိုင်ခုံ`.
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the furniture noun A1: a seat for one person, with a back, usually four legs, and sometimes two arms. UK pronunciation shown: `/tʃeə(r)/`.
- **Definition refinement:** “A seat for one person, with a back and usually four legs.”
- **Myanmar refinement:** `လူတစ်ယောက် ထိုင်ရန် ပြုလုပ်ထားပြီး ကျောမှီပါကာ ပုံမှန်အားဖြင့် ခြေထောက်လေးချောင်းပါသော ကုလားထိုင်။` The everyday Burmese headword gloss `ကုလားထိုင်` is more specific than generic `ထိုင်ခုံ`.
- **Original example proposal:** “Please move the chair closer to the window.”
- **Myanmar example:** `ကျေးဇူးပြု၍ ကုလားထိုင်ကို ပြတင်းပေါက်နား ပိုရွှေ့ပေးပါ။`
- **Grammar/editorial note:** Countable noun; “sit on a chair” is the ordinary collocation. Do not conflate the furniture noun with “chair” meaning a meeting leader or the verb “to chair a meeting”; those are separate senses/POS.

Sources:
- Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary furniture sense and A1 label: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/chair
- Cambridge UK pronunciation reference: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/chair

### 3. house — noun

- **Task / origin:** `CEFR1000-A1-0062`; source index 62.
- **Shortlist definition and meaning:** “A building in which people live.” / `အိမ်`.
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the building noun A1: a building where people live, usually one family or group. UK pronunciation shown: `/haʊs/`.
- **Definition refinement:** “A building where people live, usually one family or group.”
- **Myanmar refinement:** `လူတစ်ဦးတစ်ယောက် သို့မဟုတ် မိသားစုတစ်စု နေထိုင်လေ့ရှိသော အဆောက်အအုံ၊ အိမ်။`
- **Original example proposal:** “Their house is near the market.”
- **Myanmar example:** `သူတို့အိမ်က ဈေးနားမှာ ရှိတယ်။`
- **Grammar/editorial note:** Countable noun; the plural is `houses` with /z/ in the plural ending. Keep this building sense distinct from “the whole house” meaning the people living there and from the verb “house” meaning provide accommodation.

Sources:
- Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary building sense and A1 label: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/house
- Cambridge UK pronunciation reference: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/house

### 4. eat — verb

- **Task / origin:** `CEFR1000-A1-0074`; source index 74.
- **Shortlist definition and meaning:** “To put food in your mouth and swallow it.” / `စားသည်`.
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the food-consumption verb A1 and separately labels “to eat a meal” A1. UK pronunciation shown: `/iːt/`; past forms `ate`, `eaten`.
- **Definition refinement:** “To put food into your mouth, chew it and swallow it; also, to have a meal.”
- **Myanmar refinement:** `အစားအစာကို ပါးစပ်ထဲထည့်၊ ဝါးပြီး မျိုချသည်၊ သို့မဟုတ် အစားအစာကို စားသုံးသည်။`
- **Original example proposal:** “We eat dinner together at home.”
- **Myanmar example:** `ကျွန်တော်တို့ အိမ်မှာ ညစာကို အတူတူ စားကြတယ်။`
- **Grammar/editorial note:** Irregular verb: `eat–ate–eaten`. The shortlist’s “I eat breakfast at home” is grammatical. “Eat breakfast/dinner” is a normal meal collocation; avoid teaching a false rule that `eat` always requires a named food object.

Sources:
- Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary food and meal senses with A1 labels: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/eat
- Cambridge UK pronunciation reference: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/eat

### 5. drink — verb

- **Task / origin:** `CEFR1000-A1-0075`; source index 75.
- **Shortlist definition and meaning:** “To take a liquid into your mouth and swallow it.” / `သောက်သည်`.
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the liquid-consumption verb A1. It labels the alcohol-related verb sense A2, so the shortlist’s general liquid sense is the correct A1 target. UK pronunciation shown: `/drɪŋk/`; past forms `drank`, `drunk`.
- **Definition refinement:** “To take liquid into your mouth and swallow it.”
- **Myanmar refinement:** `အရည်တစ်မျိုးမျိုးကို ပါးစပ်ထဲထည့်ပြီး မျိုချသည်၊ သောက်သည်။`
- **Original example proposal:** “He drinks water after playing football.”
- **Myanmar example:** `သူက ဘောလုံးကစားပြီးနောက် ရေသောက်တယ်။`
- **Grammar/editorial note:** Irregular verb: `drink–drank–drunk`. The shortlist’s “They drink tea every morning” is natural. Preserve the A1 liquid sense; do not let the A2 alcohol-specific sense determine the level of this entry.

Sources:
- Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary liquid A1 and alcohol A2 distinctions: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/drink
- Cambridge UK pronunciation reference: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/drink

### 6. happy — adjective

- **Task / origin:** `CEFR1000-A1-0076`; source index 76.
- **Shortlist definition and meaning:** “Feeling pleasure or joy.” / `ပျော်ရွှင်သော`.
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels “pleased and in a good mood” A1 and also labels “making you feel happy, or expressing happiness” A1. Separate “satisfied” and “happy to do something” senses are A2. UK pronunciation shown: `/ˈhæp.i/`.
- **Definition refinement:** “Pleased and in a good mood, especially because something good has happened.”
- **Myanmar refinement:** `တစ်စုံတစ်ရာ ကောင်းမွန်သည့်အရာ ဖြစ်ပေါ်လာခြင်းကြောင့် သို့မဟုတ် အခြားအကြောင်းကြောင့် စိတ်ကျေနပ်ပျော်ရွှင်နေသော။`
- **Original example proposal:** “The children were happy to see their grandmother.”
- **Myanmar example:** `ကလေးတွေက အဖွားကို တွေ့ရလို့ ပျော်ရွှင်နေကြတယ်။`
- **Grammar/editorial note:** Predicative `be happy` is natural here. Keep the A1 emotion sense distinct from A2 `be happy to do something` (willingness) and `happy with/about` (satisfied).

Sources:
- Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary A1/A2 adjective sense distinctions: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/happy
- Cambridge UK pronunciation reference: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/happy

### 7. small — adjective

- **Task / origin:** `CEFR1000-A1-0072`; source index 72.
- **Shortlist definition and meaning:** “Not large in size.” / `သေးငယ်သော`.
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels “little in size or amount” A1 and separately labels “not important or serious” A2. UK pronunciation shown: `/smɔːl/`.
- **Definition refinement:** “Little in size or amount.”
- **Myanmar refinement:** `အရွယ်အစား သို့မဟုတ် ပမာဏအားဖြင့် ကြီးမားခြင်းမရှိသော၊ သေးငယ်သော။`
- **Original example proposal:** “They live in a small house near the river.”
- **Myanmar example:** `သူတို့က မြစ်နားက အိမ်သေးသေးလေးတစ်လုံးမှာ နေကြတယ်။`
- **Grammar/editorial note:** Attributive adjective before a noun (`a small house`). Preserve the A1 physical-size sense; the A2 “not important or serious” sense is a separate meaning.

Sources:
- Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary A1 size and A2 importance senses: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/small
- Cambridge UK pronunciation reference: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/small
