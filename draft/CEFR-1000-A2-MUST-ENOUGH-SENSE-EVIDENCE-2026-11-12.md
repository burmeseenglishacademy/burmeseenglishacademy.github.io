## A2: must (modal verb, NECESSARY)

- Manifest definition: “Used to say that something is necessary or required.”
- Manifest Myanmar: “မဖြစ်မနေ လုပ်ရမည်”.
- Manifest example: “You must wear a helmet at work.”
- Cambridge English Dictionary explicitly assigns **A2** to modal `must` in its **NECESSARY** sense; distinguishes the **PROBABLY** deduction sense as **B2**, and strong suggestion as **B1**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/must
- Cambridge Learner's Dictionary independently displays the same **A2 NECESSARY / B1 SUGGEST / B2 LIKELY** split: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/must
- Collins marks obligation/necessity modal uses **A2**: https://www.collinsdictionary.com/dictionary/english/must
- Editorial assessment: the existing workplace-helmet example expresses obligation, not probability, so the A2 placement is *supported by directly displayed source labels for this sense*. It is **not independently approved**. Myanmar “မဖြစ်မနေ လုပ်ရမည်” conveys strong necessity; reviewer should consider context-specific “...ရမည်” rather than automatically implying the same level of force in all uses.
- Proposed sense key for independent adjudication: `must|modal|necessary-obligation` (proposal only; no manifest change). Exclude epistemic deduction (“She must be tired”) from this entry; it belongs to a different sense and source level.
- Original alternative example for review (not source quotation): “Visitors must wear safety shoes inside the factory.” Myanmar draft: “စက်ရုံအတွင်း ဝင်ရောက်သူများသည် ဘေးကင်းရေးဖိနပ်များကို မဖြစ်မနေ စီးရမည်။” Original manifest example is already grammatical and needs no mandatory replacement.

## A2: enough (determiner, NECESSARY QUANTITY)

- Manifest definition: “As much or as many as needed.”
- Manifest Myanmar: “လုံလောက်သော”.
- Manifest example: “We have enough chairs for everyone.”
- Cambridge English Dictionary explicitly labels the **necessary amount/degree** use **A2**, while its **more than wanted** use is **B2** and the idiom “have had enough” is **C1**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/enough
- Cambridge Learner's Dictionary assigns **A2** to **NECESSARY**, **B2** to **WANTED**; identifies quantifier/pronoun readings: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/enough
- Cambridge English Grammar Today explicitly distinguishes determiner before noun, pronoun without noun, and adverb after adjective/adverb: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/grammar/british-grammar/enough
- Collins explicitly marks the determiner reading **A2**: https://www.collinsdictionary.com/dictionary/english/enough
- Editorial assessment: “enough chairs” is the **determiner** reading, consistent with the A2 necessary-quantity sense. The Myanmar meaning is acceptable as an adjective-like gloss but may benefit from an explanatory phrase “လိုအပ်သလောက် လုံလောက်သော” in learner-facing text; independent Myanmar review required. Do not conflate with the adverb “old enough”, pronoun “I've had enough”, or the C1 idiom.
- Proposed sense key: `enough|determiner|necessary-quantity` (proposal only).
- Original alternative example for review: “We brought enough water for the whole trip.” Myanmar draft: “ခရီးစဉ်တစ်လျှောက်အတွက် ရေကို လုံလောက်အောင် ယူလာခဲ့သည်။” The existing chairs example is grammatical and sense-aligned.
