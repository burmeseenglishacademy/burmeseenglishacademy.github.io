## Source observations (2026-11-03)

1. **a**, provisional A1, article; frozen example `I have a book.` Cambridge Learner's Dictionary explicitly labels the non-specific singular determiner sense **A1**, with British weak /ə/ and strong /eɪ/. Source: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/a . Cambridge British Grammar clarifies that the choice of **a** versus **an** depends on the next *sound*, not written letter, and that indefinite articles normally precede singular countable nouns: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/a-an . The frozen Myanmar gloss `တစ်ခု၊ တစ်ယောက် (မသတ်မှတ်ရသေးသော)` communicates indefiniteness but may encourage literal numeral translation; reviewer should verify natural teaching wording and singular-countable restrictions. Unapproved teaching candidate: `တိတိကျကျ မသတ်မှတ်ထားသော တစ်ဦး သို့မဟုတ် တစ်ခုကို ဖော်ပြရာတွင် သုံးသော article`. Original candidate example: `We saw a dog near the school.` Needs independent Myanmar and example signoff.

2. **an**, provisional A1, article; frozen example `She has an apple.` Cambridge's a/an grammar confirms the vowel-*sound* rule (e.g. **an hour**, but **a university**) and gives /ən/ in unstressed speech: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/a-an . The Cambridge learner entry for **a** explicitly lists `also an` and labels the indefinite singular sense **A1**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/a . This supports an A1 *variant of the indefinite article*, **not** an independently attested separate 200-word lexical slot. **Release-critical editorial decision:** independent reviewer must decide whether `a` and `an` count as two distinct headword/sense slots under the user-approved 200-per-level deduplication policy; do not automatically count them separately merely because written forms differ. Frozen Myanmar gloss `တစ်ခု၊ တစ်ယောက် (သရသံဖြင့်စသော စကားလုံးရှေ့တွင်)` is directionally correct, but should explicitly mean *vowel sound* rather than vowel letter. Unapproved teaching candidate: `နောက်ကလာသည့် စကားလုံး၏ အစအသံက သရသံဖြစ်လျှင် သုံးသော indefinite article`. Original candidate example: `She waited for an hour.` Needs independent review.

3. **i**, provisional A1, pronoun; frozen example `I am a student.` Cambridge Advanced Learner's Dictionary explicitly labels the first-person **subject pronoun I** as **A1** with UK /aɪ/: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/i . Cambridge distinguishes this from the **letter I** and **Roman numeral I**, which are different senses/parts of speech. **Release-critical orthography risk:** frozen headword is lowercase `i` although the English subject pronoun is conventionally capitalised `I`; retaining the lowercase headword in learner UI would teach incorrect orthography. Normalising the display form to `I` requires explicit editorial signoff and duplicate checks; do not alter frozen shortlist. Frozen Myanmar gloss `ကျွန်တော်၊ ကျွန်မ (မိမိကိုယ်ကို ရည်ညွှန်းသောစကား)` is a plausible first pass but needs review for register and subject role. Unapproved teaching candidate: `ပြောသူ သို့မဟုတ် ရေးသူကိုယ်တိုင်ကို ရည်ညွှန်းသော ကတ္တားနာမ်စား (အမြဲ I ဟု စာလုံးကြီးဖြင့်ရေးသည်)`.
