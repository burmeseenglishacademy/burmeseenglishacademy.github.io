## A2 `actually` (adverb), source index 287
Current English definition: “Used to say what is true, especially when it differs from what someone expected.”
Current Myanmar: “တကယ်တော့၊ အမှန်တကယ်”
Current example: “I thought it was Tuesday, but it is actually Wednesday.”

**Evidence:** Cambridge Advanced Learner's Dictionary identifies **A2** `actually` = *in fact/really*, but **B1** `actually` for surprising or contrary-to-expectation information. Cambridge Learner's Dictionary additionally identifies **B2** for disagreeing/correcting a claim. The provisional English definition explicitly introduces expectation contrast and its example corrects a mistaken day; therefore A2 cannot be treated as demonstrated merely because the headword has an A2 sense. Exact example pragmatics require reviewer judgment.

Sources observed 2026-11-21:
- https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/actually (A2 IN FACT; B1 SURPRISE)
- https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/actually (A2 TRUTH; B1 SURPRISE; B2 MISTAKE)
- https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/essential-british-english/actually (A2 truth; B1 surprise/correction)

**Author-only A2 candidate**, subject to independent approval:
- English: “In fact; used to say what is really true.”
- Myanmar candidate: “အမှန်တကယ်၊ တကယ်ပဲ” (review register and naturalness; distinguish discourse-marker “တကယ်တော့”).
- Original example: “I didn't actually see the bird; I only heard it.” The truth-versus-assumption contrast may still need careful sense assessment; reviewer should choose an unequivocal A2 example, not simply accept this suggestion.

## B2 `hardly` (adverb), source index 147
Current English definition: “Almost not at all.”
Current Myanmar: “မ…သလောက်၊ လုံးဝနီးပါး မ…”
Current example: “I could hardly hear the speaker.”

**Evidence:** Cambridge Advanced Learner's Dictionary explicitly labels **B1** `hardly` meaning *only just / almost not*, including the construction `could hardly hear`. Its **B2** sense is *certainly not*, used to challenge the plausibility of a claim. Cambridge Learner's Dictionary confirms B1 NOT MUCH versus B2 NOT LIKELY. The current definition and example are aligned with **B1**, not the selected B2 sense. This is a substantive sense-level conflict; do not infer that the B2 inventory is valid from the headword alone.

Sources observed 2026-11-21:
- https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/hardly (B1 ONLY JUST; B2 CERTAINLY NOT)
- https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/hardly (B1 NOT MUCH; B2 NOT LIKELY)

**Author-only B2 candidate**, subject to independent approval:
- English: “Used to say that something is certainly not true, reasonable or likely.”
- Myanmar candidate: “အဲဒီလိုတော့ မဟုတ်နိုင်ဘူး၊ …လို့တော့ မဆိုနိုင်ဘူး” (context-dependent; independently review for fluent Myanmar).
- Original example: “You can hardly expect everyone to agree immediately.” This is a different sense from “I could hardly hear”; reviewer must verify its exact B2 use.
