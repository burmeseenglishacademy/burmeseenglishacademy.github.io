## 1. personality — B2 sense supported

**Selected sense:** a person's character, expressed through the way they behave, feel and think.

Cambridge's Learner's Dictionary labels the character sense **B2** and defines it as the way a person is. The main Cambridge English Dictionary labels the corresponding character sense **B2 [C or U]**. This matches the shortlist's intended meaning, “the qualities of character that make a person different from others.” The dictionary also has a separate B2 sense meaning a famous person; that is not the sense selected for this entry.

- **UK pronunciation:** /ˌpɜː.səˈnæl.ə.ti/
- **Myanmar refinement for reviewer consideration:** လူတစ်ဦး၏ အပြုအမူ၊ ခံစားချက်နှင့် အတွေးအခေါ်တို့မှ ပေါ်လွင်လာသော ကိုယ်ရည်ကိုယ်သွေး၊ စရိုက်လက္ခဏာများ။
- **Current example:** “Her friendly personality helps her make new friends.”
- **Grammar assessment:** Correct. “Friendly” modifies the noun; “helps + object + bare infinitive” is grammatical. The example is understandable and fits the selected character sense.
- **Possible fresh alternative:** “His calm personality helps him deal with difficult customers.”
- **Editorial recommendation:** Keep B2 as a well-supported provisional placement, subject to independent verification of the Myanmar gloss and example originality.

## 2. relief — B2 emotional sense supported; definition should be narrowed

**Selected sense:** the positive feeling experienced when something unpleasant has ended or has not happened.

Cambridge's Learner's Dictionary labels the emotion sense **B2**. The main Cambridge English Dictionary labels the happiness sense **B2 [S or U]**. The shortlist's example clearly illustrates this emotional sense. However, the current English definition also mentions pain, which can blur the entry with the separate physical-relief sense. The main dictionary separately labels the help/aid sense **C2**; that is not the intended meaning here.

- **UK pronunciation:** /rɪˈliːf/
- **Suggested narrower English definition:** “A feeling of happiness or comfort because something unpleasant has ended or did not happen.”
- **Myanmar refinement for reviewer consideration:** မနှစ်မြို့ဖွယ်အရာတစ်ခု ပြီးဆုံးသွားခြင်း သို့မဟုတ် မဖြစ်ပေါ်ခဲ့ခြင်းကြောင့် ခံစားရသော စိတ်သက်သာမှု၊ စိတ်အေးချမ်းမှု။
- **Current example:** “He felt relief when the missing bag was found.”
- **Grammar assessment:** Correct and natural. “Felt relief” uses the uncountable abstract noun naturally; the when-clause gives the cause of the feeling.
- **Possible fresh alternative:** “When the missing bag was found, he felt a deep sense of relief.”
- **Editorial recommendation:** Retain B2 provisionally, but narrow the definition to the emotional sense and do not let the C2 aid/assistance sense or the physical-pain sense leak into this entry.

Sources:
- https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/relief
- https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/relief

## 3. resistance — B2 placement is not substantiated by the inspected CEFR evidence

**Selected sense:** opposition to a change, proposal or action; unwillingness to accept it.

The Cambridge Learner's Dictionary has a “DISAGREE” sense matching this meaning, but the indexed learner entry does not display a CEFR label for it. The main Cambridge English Dictionary labels the broad noun sense covering fighting against an attack or refusing to accept something **C2 [U]**. This is a material challenge to the provisional B2 placement, but it should not be converted automatically into a definitive C2 reassignment: the learner entry's exact sense is unlabelled, and a second independent level source is still needed.

- **UK pronunciation:** /rɪˈzɪs.təns/
- **Suggested narrower English definition:** “Opposition to a change, idea or action; a refusal to accept it.”
- **Myanmar refinement for reviewer consideration:** ပြောင်းလဲမှု၊ အကြံအစည် သို့မဟုတ် လုပ်ဆောင်ချက်တစ်ခုကို လက်မခံဘဲ ဆန့်ကျင်ခြင်း၊ ခုခံဆန့်ကျင်မှု။
- **Current example:** “The proposal met resistance from local residents.”
- **Grammar assessment:** Correct. “Meet resistance from” is a natural collocation; “from local residents” identifies the source of the opposition.
- **Possible fresh alternative:** “Several employees expressed resistance to the proposed schedule change.”
- **Editorial recommendation:** Keep the entry on hold. Do not claim B2 evidence from the inspected Cambridge pages. Obtain another explicit CEFR source or have an independent reviewer recommend reallocation/removal.

Sources:
- https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/resistance

## 4. retail — direct C1 conflict with the provisional B2 placement

**Selected sense:** the activity/business of selling goods directly to the public, usually through shops.

The Cambridge English Dictionary labels the noun sense **C1**. This is the same retail activity sense represented by the shortlist definition and example. The provisional B2 placement is therefore directly challenged by a source that explicitly labels the selected noun sense C1.

- **UK pronunciation:** /ˈriː.teɪl/
- **Suggested English definition:** “The activity or business of selling goods directly to members of the public, usually in shops.”
- **Myanmar refinement for reviewer consideration:** အများပြည်သူထံ ကုန်ပစ္စည်းများကို လက်လီရောင်းချသည့် လုပ်ငန်း၊ လက်လီရောင်းချရေး။
- **Current example:** “She has worked in retail for five years.”
- **Grammar assessment:** Correct and natural. “Retail” is used as an uncountable industry/activity noun after “in”; “for five years” correctly expresses duration.
- **Possible fresh alternative:** “The company plans to expand its retail operations next year.”
- **Editorial recommendation:** Treat B2 as a level conflict requiring independent resolution. Consider this candidate for C1 allocation only after reviewing the complete 200-word C1 set and its replacement candidate; do not move it automatically or change the shortlist in this research note.
