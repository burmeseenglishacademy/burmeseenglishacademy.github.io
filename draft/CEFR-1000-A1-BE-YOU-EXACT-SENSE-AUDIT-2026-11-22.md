## CEFR1000-A1-0003 — be (verb), source index 2
- Current English: “To exist or to have a particular quality or identity.”
- Current Myanmar: “ဖြစ်သည်၊ ရှိသည်”
- Current example: “I want to be a teacher.”
- Cambridge Learner's Dictionary labels **be — DESCRIBE** A1 and **there is/are — existence** A1; these are distinct constructions. https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/be?q=be_1
- Cambridge Grammar documents main-verb and auxiliary-verb functions and the present forms am/is/are. This is **grammar evidence, not a separate CEFR assignment**. https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/be-
- **Issue:** Current definition merges existence with identity/quality. The example illustrates a desired future occupation, not the existential “there is” construction. A reviewer should choose the precise target sense rather than assume the two are interchangeable.
- Candidate English (identity/description): “Used to say what someone or something is, or to describe them.”
- Candidate Myanmar: “လူတစ်ယောက် သို့မဟုတ် အရာတစ်ခု၏ အခြေအနေ၊ အရည်အသွေး၊ အမျိုးအစား သို့မဟုတ် မည်သူမည်ဝါဖြစ်ကြောင်း ဖော်ပြရာတွင် သုံးသော ကြိယာ။”
- Candidate original example: “My sister is a doctor.” (identity/profession; singular noun article present)
- If existence is retained, make a separate sense/construction and independently verify it; do not silently expand this entry.

## CEFR1000-A1-0005 — you (pronoun), source index 4
- Current English: “The person or people someone is speaking to.”
- Current Myanmar: “သင်၊ မင်း၊ ရှင်”
- Current example: “You are my friend.”
- Cambridge Learner's Dictionary explicitly labels **person spoken to** A1, but **people generally** A2: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/you
- Cambridge British Grammar confirms that **you** is singular or plural, and both subject and object; this is grammar evidence, not an independent CEFR label: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/uk/grammar/british-grammar/pronouns-personal-it
- **Issue:** The provisional entry correctly targets direct address, but its Myanmar gloss gives only singular-style address options and is register-dependent. Avoid interpreting the generic A2 meaning as part of the A1 sense.
- Candidate English: “The person or people being spoken or written to.”
- Candidate Myanmar: “စကားပြောနေသူ သို့မဟုတ် စာရေးသူက တိုက်ရိုက် ရည်ညွှန်းပြောဆိုနေသည့် လူတစ်ဦး သို့မဟုတ် လူအများကို ဆိုလိုသော နာမ်စား။ ‘မင်း၊ ရှင်၊ သင်၊ ခင်ဗျား၊ သင်တို့’ စသည်ဖြင့် အခြေအနေနှင့် ယဉ်ကျေးမှုအဆင့်အလိုက် ဘာသာပြန်နိုင်သည်။”
- Candidate original example: “Are you ready for class?” (direct address, subject); optional separate illustration “I can help you.” (object)
- Do not promote the generic “people in general” sense as A1 without distinct evidence.
