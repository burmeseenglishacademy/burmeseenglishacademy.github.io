## Publisher evidence, exact senses and levels
1. Cambridge Dictionary, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/have (observed 2026-11-08): `have` **verb (POSSESS)** explicitly **A1**, transitive and normally non-continuous, meaning ownership/possession; distinguishes `have` **auxiliary** for perfect tenses as **A2** and causative `have` as **B1**. British strong /hæv/, weak /həv/ and /əv/ are displayed, but weak forms primarily apply to auxiliary contexts; do not mechanically use weak auxiliary pronunciations for a stressed possessive headword.
2. Cambridge Essential British English, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/essential-british-english/have (observed 2026-11-08): explicitly labels the possession/family main-verb sense **A1**, and perfect-tense auxiliary **A2**; also labels illness and eating/drinking uses A1. **Do not conflate these different main-verb senses** merely because they share a level.
3. Oxford Advanced Learner's Dictionary, https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/have_1 (observed 2026-11-08): British-English possession commonly uses `have got`; `do you have` and `don't have` are also accepted. This supports usage notes, not independent evidence of a particular CEFR label.

## Editorial decision required
The inventory example **“I have two sisters.”** expresses family relationship rather than strict legal ownership. Although Cambridge Essential British English explicitly groups family relations under A1 `have`, the inventory definition “To own or possess something” is too narrow to explain its own example. **Do not teach that people are owned.** Proposed narrow A1 teaching sense: “To own something, or to say what family members or personal features someone has.” Suggested fluent Myanmar teaching explanation: “ပစ္စည်းတစ်ခုခု ပိုင်ဆိုင်ထားခြင်း၊ သို့မဟုတ် မိသားစုဝင်နှင့် ကိုယ်ပိုင်လက္ခဏာများ ရှိကြောင်း ပြောရာတွင် သုံးသည်။” These are author proposals, not independently validated translations.

Original proposed examples (not copied from publisher): 
- Possession: **I have a blue bag.** — **ကျွန်တော့်မှာ အပြာရောင်အိတ်တစ်လုံး ရှိတယ်။**
- Family: **She has one older brother.** — **သူ့မှာ အစ်ကိုတစ်ယောက် ရှိတယ်။**

Grammar/voice checkpoints: third-person singular `has`; past `had`; `I have a bag`, not `I am having a bag` for ordinary possession. British George/Emma headword and full-sentence audio require actual generation, file hashes, and human listening evidence; nothing was generated or listened to here.
