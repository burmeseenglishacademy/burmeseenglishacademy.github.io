2. Cambridge Essential British English, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/essential-british-english/fan — independently displayed dictionary entry also labels **A2** for an admirer and **B1** for an air-moving object. Both are Cambridge publications, not two independent human reviewers.

**Finding:** The shortlist's `fan` noun meaning (air-moving device) is consistent with the source-labelled **B1 object sense**. This is a useful *positive-control* entry: a naive headword-only lookup could incorrectly assign A2 from the admirer sense. The original English definition is accurate but could distinguish electric and handheld forms more clearly. Do not convert this observation into a reviewed flag or human signoff.

## Editorial candidate (not an approved replacement)
- **Exact sense:** A machine or handheld object that moves air, often to make someone feel cooler.
- **Myanmar:** ပန်ကာ၊ လေတိုက်စေရန် အသုံးပြုသော ကိရိယာ။ For an electric device, `လျှပ်စစ်ပန်ကာ` is more specific. `ပရိသတ်` or `အားပေးသူ` would mean an admirer and is WRONG for this entry.
- **New original example:** “The office was hot, so we switched on the fan.”
- **Myanmar example translation candidate:** “ရုံးခန်းထဲမှာ ပူနေလို့ ကျွန်တော်တို့ ပန်ကာကို ဖွင့်လိုက်တယ်။”
- **Grammar check:** `fan` is a singular count noun with `the`; `switched on` is transitive with the particle before its noun object. The Myanmar sentence preserves past action and causal relationship.
- **Potential audio check:** British George and Emma must pronounce the *noun* in both the isolated headword and the full sentence; spelling alone cannot prove correct audio. No synthesis or listening was performed for this dossier.
