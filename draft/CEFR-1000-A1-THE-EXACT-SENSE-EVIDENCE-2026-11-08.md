## Publisher evidence
- Cambridge English Dictionary, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/the (observed **2026-11-08**): `the` determiner **(PARTICULAR)** explicitly marked **A1** when a referent is already mentioned, known or identifiable in context; other A1 contexts include restricted noun phrases and unique referents. The **superlative/ordinal** use is separately marked **A2**, and possessive-replacement **(YOUR)** is **B1**. Do not infer one CEFR level for every sense of the headword.
- Same publisher lists British weak /ðə/ and strong /ðiː/. The weak form normally precedes a consonant sound, and /ði/ commonly precedes a vowel sound; emphasis can change pronunciation. British George/Emma headword recordings must not be treated as sufficient to test contextual weak forms in example audio.
- Cambridge grammar guidance on articles is linked from the same entry; distinguish indefinite `a/an` from identifiable `the`. The publisher's examples are evidence only and are not reused here.
