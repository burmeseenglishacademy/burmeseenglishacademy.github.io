## A2 project (noun; source index 595, A2-C1 starter blob b04dd85db5cfae131c25aa4c73109778991b6fdc)

Provisional definition: “A planned piece of work with a particular goal.” Myanmar: “စီမံကိန်း၊ လုပ်ငန်းအစီအစဉ်”. Example: “Our class project is about saving water.”

Cambridge Advanced Learner's Dictionary explicitly distinguishes **A2** student research/study project from **B2** general planned work or activity. Its British noun pronunciation is /ˈprɒdʒ.ekt/, whereas verb *project* is /prəˈdʒekt/. Publisher: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/project (observed 2026-11-06). Collins independently distinguishes a pupil's study from a longer task but the accessed result did not provide a CEFR level: https://www.collinsdictionary.com/english-language-learning/project (observed 2026-11-06).

**Concrete discrepancy:** A2 example matches the Cambridge school-study sense, but the generic definition and Myanmar rendering also cover Cambridge's B2 work-project sense. Suggested *unapproved* exact-sense rewrite: “A piece of schoolwork in which students study a subject over time.” Myanmar candidate: “ကျောင်းသားများက အကြောင်းအရာတစ်ခုကို အချိန်ယူ လေ့လာပြုစုရသည့် သင်ခန်းစာဆိုင်ရာ စီမံလုပ်ငန်း”. Keep original example if independent grammar/translation reviewer agrees. If editorial intent is a workplace project, A2 is not publisher-supported for that sense and a replacement/rebalancing decision is required. George/Emma noun audio must not be generated using verb stress.

## A1 minute (noun; source index 147, A1 starter blob ff1e93f60828ef2f94c9146522ce94075183b0ae)

Provisional definition: “A period of sixty seconds.” Myanmar: “မိနစ်”. Example: “Wait one minute, please.”

Cambridge Advanced Learner's Dictionary labels the sixty-second noun **A1**, short-time colloquial noun **A2**, and tiny adjective **C2**; UK noun /ˈmɪn.ɪt/, UK adjective /maɪˈnjuːt/. Publisher: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/minute (observed 2026-11-06). Cambridge Essential British also confirms A1 for the sixty-second unit and A2 for a short time: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/essential-british-english/minute (observed 2026-11-06).

**Concrete discrepancy:** “Wait one minute, please” may pragmatically mean “wait a short time” (Cambridge A2) rather than exactly sixty seconds (A1). Proposed *unapproved* sense-unambiguous original example: “There are sixty seconds in one minute.” Myanmar candidate: “တစ်မိနစ်တွင် စက္ကန့်ခြောက်ဆယ်ရှိသည်။” Requires independent bilingual and grammar review. Audio should use noun stress and not adjective /maɪˈnjuːt/.
