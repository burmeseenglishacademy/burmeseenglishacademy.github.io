## B2 `prepare` — concrete lower-level conflict

- Manifest: verb; “To make someone or something ready for an activity.” Myanmar: “ပြင်ဆင်သည်”. Example: “We need to prepare the documents before the meeting.”
- Cambridge Learner's Dictionary [prepare](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/prepare), **GET READY A2**, specifically covers getting someone/something ready for a future event; separate `prepare yourself` for difficult circumstances is **B1**. Cambridge English Dictionary [prepare](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/prepare?q=prepare_1) likewise labels the basic transitive sense **A2**.
- Collins [prepare](https://www.collinsdictionary.com/dictionary/english/prepare) labels the transitive ready-making sense **A2** and a distinct preparing-for-event construction **B1**.
- Assessment: the provisional definition and example directly instantiate the **A2** transitive ready-making sense, **not B2**. Do not treat a word-level label as covering all senses. Editorial disposition: **B2 placement conflict — replace with a demonstrably B2 exact sense or replace the headword**, maintaining 200 B2 entries and cross-level uniqueness after independent adjudication.
- Myanmar: “ပြင်ဆင်သည်” is natural for preparing documents; no correction is necessary solely for fluency. Suggested original replacement example for the *same A2 sense*, **not a B2 fix**: “The staff prepared the room before the meeting.”

## B2 `calm` — concrete lower-level conflict

- Manifest: adjective; “Not worried, excited or upset.” Myanmar: “တည်ငြိမ်သော”. Example: “She remained calm during the emergency.”
- Collins [calm](https://www.collinsdictionary.com/dictionary/english/calm) explicitly labels the **adjective describing a person without worry, anger or excitement B1**. The Cambridge [calm](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/calm) intermediate-English entry describes the same emotion-related adjective, but that section alone does not establish a CEFR rating.
- Assessment: Collins **B1** matches the provisional meaning and the emotional-state example. B2 placement is **conflicted**, not verified. The weather/sea adjective, noun and verb must not be silently substituted for the emotional adjective.
- Myanmar: “တည်ငြိမ်သော” is reasonable; for the emotional sense “စိတ်တည်ငြိမ်သော” is more explicit. Original alternative example: “Despite the sudden delay, the supervisor stayed calm.” This remains an editorial suggestion, not independent validation.

## B2 `client` — sense-level support, independent signoff still missing

- Manifest: noun; “A person or organisation that pays for professional services.” Myanmar: “ဖောက်သည်”. Example: “The consultant met a new client.”
- Cambridge English Dictionary [client](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/client) labels **client noun (CUSTOMER) B2** and distinguishes a separate computing sense without the same visible CEFR label.
- Assessment: the professional-services noun in the example aligns with the Cambridge B2 customer/service sense. Cambridge also includes recipients of services, so the manifest's “pays” wording may be overly restrictive. Suggested definition: “A person or organisation that receives services from a professional or business.” Keep the computing sense separate.
