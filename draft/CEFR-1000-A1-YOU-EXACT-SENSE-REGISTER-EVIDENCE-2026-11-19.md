## Sources observed 2026-11-19
1. Cambridge Advanced Learner's Dictionary, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/you — **A1** specifically for **PERSON/PEOPLE ADDRESSED**, but **A2** for **PEOPLE GENERALLY**. This is an exact-sense distinction, not blanket A1 for every use of *you*.
2. Cambridge Learner's Dictionary, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/you — confirms A1 for person spoken to and A2 for generic people.
3. British Council LearnEnglish, https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/personal-pronouns — documents *you* as both subject and object; generic *you* also exists. Grammar guidance supports usage, not independent lexical CEFR approval.

## Provisional text under inspection
- English definition: “The person or people someone is speaking to.”
- Myanmar: “သင်၊ မင်း၊ ရှင်”
- Example: “You are my friend.”

## Editorial assessment and proposed original wording (not approved)
- **Exact sense:** second-person pronoun referring to one or more people directly addressed, **A1 candidate supported by Cambridge**. Exclude the generic-people A2 sense from this A1 entry.
- **Proposed English definition:** “A word used for the person or people you are speaking to.”
- **Proposed Myanmar meaning:** “စကားပြောနေသူက မိမိပြောဆိုနေသည့် တစ်ဦး သို့မဟုတ် တစ်ဦးထက်ပိုသောသူများကို ရည်ညွှန်းရာတွင် သုံးသော နာမ်စား (ဥပမာ—သင်၊ ရှင်၊ မင်း၊ နင်)။” Register is contextual: `သင်` may sound formal/bookish, `ရှင်` is context-sensitive and `မင်း/နင်` can be informal or inappropriate depending on relationship. No single Myanmar form is universally neutral.
- **Original example (singular addressee):** “You are welcome to sit here.”
- **Proposed Myanmar example:** “ဒီမှာ ထိုင်လို့ရပါတယ်နော်။” Natural Myanmar may omit an explicit second-person pronoun; independent reviewers must assess whether an instructional translation should explicitly show the pronoun or use a natural omission.
- **Original example (plural addressees):** “You can all wait in this room.”
- **Proposed Myanmar example:** “အားလုံး ဒီအခန်းထဲမှာ စောင့်နေလို့ရပါတယ်။”
- **Original contrasting A2 generic example:** “You never know what will happen tomorrow.” Here *you* can mean people generally; **do not** use this as proof of A1 exact sense.
