## Publisher evidence inspected 2026-11-08
1. Cambridge Learner's Dictionary, `a` determiner, BEFORE NOUN explicitly **A1**, UK weak /ə/, strong /eɪ/: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/a . Other A1 senses include ONE, EVERY/EACH and TYPE, while AN ACTION and TWO NOUNS are A2. **Only the indefinite first-mention singular referent sense is in scope here.** Cambridge records `an` as an alternative form of the same determiner, rather than necessarily a separately CEFR-ranked lexical sense.
2. Cambridge English Grammar Today, “A/an and the”: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/a-an-and-the . A before consonant **sounds** and an before vowel **sounds**; singular count nouns, not ordinary plural or mass nouns; examples of sound/spelling mismatch include *a university* and *an hour*. This is grammatical support, **not an independent explicit A1 CEFR rating for `an` as a distinct headword**.
3. British Council, “Specific and general determiners”: https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/specific-general-determiners . Describes a/an as a general determiner with singular nouns. This supports sense and usage, not a separate publisher CEFR headword rating.

## Editorial decisions to refer for independent review
- **Potential counting conflict:** `a` and `an` are phonologically conditioned forms of one indefinite article, not independent lexical senses. The approved 200 A1 *deduplicated headword/sense* requirement may be violated if they count as two distinct vocabulary targets. Do not silently merge/remove them: independent curriculum decision required, with replacement and total recount if merged.
- **Exact-sense CEFR gap:** `a` first-mention sense has explicit Cambridge A1 evidence. `an` shares the grammatical article but **separate headword A1 classification remains unproven**; do not infer one from a generic grammar article.
- **Phonological rule:** the next pronounced sound, not the first letter of the noun, controls form; an intervening adjective matters (*an old book*, *a useful book*).
- **Myanmar:** an indefinite article often has no separate natural Myanmar translation; teaching it mechanically as “တစ်ခု” in every sentence creates unnatural translations. Proposed explanation (not independently approved): `a` — “မည်သည့်အရာ/လူကို ဆိုလိုသည်ဟု အတိအကျ မသတ်မှတ်ထားသည့် တစ်ခုကိန်း ရေတွက်နိုင်သော နာမ်ရှေ့တွင် သုံးသည့် စကားလုံး။ နောက်စကားလုံး၏ အသံသည် ဗျည်းသံဖြင့် စသည်။” `an` — “`a` နှင့် အဓိပ္ပာယ်တူပြီး နောက်စကားလုံး၏ အသံသည် သရသံဖြင့် စသည့်အခါ သုံးသော ပုံစံ။”
