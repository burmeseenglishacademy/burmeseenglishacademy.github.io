## Candidate evidence and editorial findings

### 1. mother — A1 noun, parent sense

- **Task:** `CEFR1000-A1-0025`; original source index 24.
- **Cambridge evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the noun sense “your female parent” A1. The entry also separates the capitalised title for an important nun; the shortlist’s lowercase common noun does not select that title.
- **Source:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/mother
- **Current English definition:** “A female parent.” This is concise and exact for the selected sense; retain provisionally.
- **Myanmar meaning:** `အမေ၊ မိခင်` is appropriate. `အမေ` is conversational and `မိခင်` is more formal.
- **Current original example:** “My mother is kind.”
- **Grammar:** Subject + possessive determiner + singular count noun + linking verb + adjective. The sentence is grammatical and uses the intended parent sense.
- **Suggested Myanmar rendering:** `ကျွန်တော့်အမေက ကြင်နာတတ်တယ်။` / `ကျွန်မအမေက ကြင်နာတတ်တယ်။` The speaker form should be selected consistently with the course’s chosen Burmese voice/register; `အမေက ကြင်နာတတ်တယ်။` is possible where the possessor is clear from context.
- **Sense boundary:** Do not transfer the A1 parent label to the separate religious title or food-fermentation uses.

### 2. father — A1 noun, parent sense

- **Task:** `CEFR1000-A1-0026`; original source index 25.
- **Cambridge evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the noun meaning “your male parent” A1. It separately lists the religious title “Father” and a formal verb; these are not the selected lowercase parent noun.
- **Source:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/father
- **Current English definition:** “A male parent.” Concise and exact for the selected sense; retain provisionally.
- **Myanmar meaning:** `အဖေ၊ ဖခင်` is appropriate; `အဖေ` is conversational and `ဖခင်` is more formal.
- **Current original example:** “My father works here.”
- **Grammar:** Subject + possessive determiner + singular count noun + present-simple verb with third-person singular `-s` + place adverb. Correct and suitable as a short A1 model.
- **Suggested Myanmar rendering:** `ကျွန်တော့်အဖေက ဒီမှာ အလုပ်လုပ်တယ်။` / `ကျွန်မအဖေက ဒီမှာ အလုပ်လုပ်တယ်။`
- **Sense boundary:** The parent sense is the only one supported here as A1. Do not infer a CEFR level for the priest-title or formal verb from the A1 parent label.

### 3. school — A1 noun, educational institution/place sense

- **Task:** `CEFR1000-A1-0027`; original source index 26.
- **Cambridge evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the place where children go to be educated A1. It labels the “time that you spend at school” sense A2. The broader Cambridge English Dictionary also separates the A1 institution/place and A2 daytime-period senses.
- **Sources:**
  - https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/school
  - https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/school
- **Current English definition:** “A place where children learn.” Understandable, but a closer learner-friendly alignment is **“A place where children go to learn.”**
- **Myanmar meaning:** `ကျောင်း` is accurate for the institution/place sense.
- **Current original example:** “Our school is near my home.”
- **Grammar:** Singular subject noun phrase with possessive determiner + present-simple `be` + adjective/preposition phrase `near my home`. Correct.
- **Suggested Myanmar rendering:** `ကျွန်တော်တို့ကျောင်းက ကျွန်တော့်အိမ်နားမှာ ရှိတယ်။` / `ကျွန်မတို့ကျောင်းက ကျွန်မအိမ်နားမှာ ရှိတယ်။`
- **Cross-level safeguard:** Keep the A1 place/institution sense distinct from the A2 “period or time spent at school” sense. The shortlist’s example selects the A1 place sense.

### 4. teacher — A1 noun, person whose job is to teach

- **Task:** `CEFR1000-A1-0028`; original source index 27.
- **Cambridge evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels A1 the noun meaning someone whose job is to teach in a school, college, etc.
- **Source:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/teacher
- **Current English definition:** “A person who helps students learn.” This is understandable but broader than the occupational sense. Suggested tighter definition: **“A person whose job is to teach in a school, college, or similar place.”**
- **Myanmar meaning:** `ဆရာ၊ ဆရာမ` is appropriate; use both forms so the headword is not restricted to one gender.
- **Current original example:** “The teacher is friendly.”
- **Grammar:** Definite article + singular count noun + present-simple linking verb + adjective complement. Correct and simple.
- **Suggested Myanmar rendering:** `ဆရာ/ဆရာမက ဖော်ရွေတယ်။`
- **Sense boundary:** The definition should retain the “job of teaching” feature; merely helping someone learn is not sufficiently precise.

### 5. student — A1 noun, person studying at an educational institution

- **Task:** `CEFR1000-A1-0029`; original source index 28.
- **Cambridge evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels A1 someone studying at a school or university.
- **Source:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/student
- **Current English definition:** “A person who studies at a school or college.” This is close. For alignment with the cited entry, suggested wording: **“A person who studies at a school or university.”**
- **Myanmar meaning:** `ကျောင်းသား၊ ကျောင်းသူ` is appropriate for male and female students. In explanatory prose, `ကျောင်းတက်နေသူ` can describe a student without specifying gender.
- **Current original example:** “She is a good student.”
- **Grammar:** Subject pronoun + present-simple linking verb + indefinite article + adjective + singular count noun. Correct.
- **Suggested Myanmar rendering:** `သူမက ကျောင်းသူကောင်းတစ်ယောက်ပါ။` Avoid translating `good` specifically as “academically clever” unless that narrower meaning is intended.
- **Sense boundary:** The cited A1 sense includes school and university students; do not narrow it to children or school pupils only.
