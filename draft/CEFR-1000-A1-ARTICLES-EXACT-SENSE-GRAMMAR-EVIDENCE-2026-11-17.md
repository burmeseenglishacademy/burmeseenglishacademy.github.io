## Sources and observed support
1. Cambridge Dictionary, *English Grammar Today*, “A/an and the”: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/a-and-the — indefinite vs definite reference; choice of a/an depends on initial **sound**; `the` varies in pronunciation before vowel/consonant sounds.

## Sense, grammar, and Myanmar QA observations
- The current `a` definition omits the consonant-*sound* rule and singular countability; `an` includes the sound rule but omits countability. These are **scope gaps**, not proof of a wrong CEFR level.
- `a university` is grammatical despite the initial vowel letter because the spoken first sound is consonantal; `an hour` is grammatical despite the initial consonant letter because the `h` is silent. Both examples are newly authored here.
- `the` is not simply “one particular thing”: it can also refer to plural and uncountable noun phrases. The candidate avoids limiting it to singular nouns.
- Burmese has no exact one-word, always-obligatory article equivalent. The proposed Myanmar lines are **explanations of function**, not literal word-for-word translations; an independent fluent Myanmar reviewer must assess naturalness, register, readability, and terminology.
- Original examples: `She` subject + `is` copula + `a university student` complement; `We` subject + `waited` verb + `for an hour` duration adjunct; `Please close` imperative + `the window beside you` identifiable object.
- For George/Emma British audio, verify actual rendered `a/an/the` both in isolation and connected example speech. Grammar guidance on the variants of `the` does **not** constitute generated audio or listening signoff.
