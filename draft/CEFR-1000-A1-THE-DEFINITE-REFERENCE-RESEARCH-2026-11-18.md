## Sense alignment and risks
- The current definition excludes plural and uncountable referents by saying “person or thing” in singular terms. It also underexplains that specificity is contextual: previously mentioned, mutually identifiable, or unique. A1 teaching should avoid falsely implying every noun with `the` is objectively unique.
- “The book is on the table” is grammatical but provides no context for why the listener knows which book/table; as an isolated sentence it does not demonstrate the difference between first mention and subsequent reference. It is not wrong, merely pedagogically underdetermined.
- Myanmar has no obligatory article directly corresponding to English `the`. A standalone Burmese gloss that sounds like a word-for-word translation could mislead learners. Explain the function rather than inventing a fixed Burmese equivalent.
- The English headword `the` is distinct from `a/an`, but a review of the three article entries should consider learner-facing cross-references, not silently merge them. General-category `the`, superlatives, geographic names, and institution expressions are distinct extensions that need separate source-backed decisions.

- Original contextual example: “I bought a notebook yesterday. The notebook is blue.” Myanmar explanation: “မနေ့က မှတ်စုစာအုပ်တစ်အုပ် ဝယ်ခဲ့တယ်။ အဲဒီမှတ်စုစာအုပ်က အပြာရောင်ပါ။” The second mention makes reference explicit.
