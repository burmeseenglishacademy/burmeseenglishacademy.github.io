## Source observations (exact lexical sense, not blanket headword certification)
1. Cambridge Learner's Dictionary, `a` determiner, BEFORE NOUN, **A1**: one not-yet-identified singular referent. Also labels ONE, EVERY/EACH and TYPE A1; labels AN ACTION, TWO NOUNS and AMOUNTS A2. https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/a
2. Cambridge Advanced Learner's Dictionary, `an` determiner, **A1**, alternate form of `a` before a vowel **sound**, not merely vowel letter. https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/an
3. Cambridge English Grammar Today, British grammar: articles are determiners; `a` precedes a consonant **sound**, `an` a vowel **sound**, and only singular countable nouns accept ordinary indefinite `a/an`. https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/a-an-and-the

**Teaching caveat:** The examples demonstrate distinct form rules; `a/an` is frequently not translated as a separate Myanmar word. Avoid presenting `တစ်ခု` as a mandatory one-to-one translation. For beginners, explain the noun phrase rather than asserting an English-style article category exists in Myanmar.

**Critical contrasts:** `a university` (initial /j/ consonant sound), `an hour` (silent h), `an umbrella`, `a useful tool`. `a water` is not the ordinary mass-noun construction; teach `a glass of water`. Do not generalise all Cambridge `a` senses to A1: e.g. action-noun constructions can be A2. The `an` entry is a form-conditioned variant, not a separate meaning; an independent editor must decide whether separate headword records are pedagogically justified and how deduplication should treat them.
