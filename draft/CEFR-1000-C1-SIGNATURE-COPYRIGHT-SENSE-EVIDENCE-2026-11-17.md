## C1 provisional: signature (noun)
- Shortlist global zero-based index 803; original source index 75. Current sense: a person's handwritten name; current Myanmar: `လက်မှတ်ရေးထိုးချက်`; example: “Please add your signature at the bottom of the form.”
- Source: Cambridge Learner's Dictionary, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/signature (observed 2026-11-17). **Explicit B1** label for the handwritten-name noun sense. Corroboration: Cambridge Essential British English, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/essential-british-english/signature (observed 2026-11-17), also **B1**.
- Finding: strong source-backed C1-vs-B1 conflict for the *exact currently written sense*, not merely the headword. Do not count this as independently resolved; require a human reviewer to choose reassignment/replacement consistent with exactly 200 genuinely reviewed senses at each level. No automatic migration or promotion.
- Suggested precise English: “A person's name written by that person in a characteristic way, especially to sign a document.” Suggested Myanmar: `စာရွက်စာတမ်းတစ်ခုကို အတည်ပြုရန် မိမိကိုယ်တိုင် ရေးထိုးထားသော လက်မှတ်` (editorial proposal, not native-speaker signoff). Original example proposal: “Please sign here; we need your signature before we can process the form.” The example targets the noun, not the verb “sign”.
- Sense trap: `signature dish` is adjectival and cannot justify keeping the noun's handwritten-name sense at C1. Cambridge English dictionary lists specialized characteristic and adjective uses separately: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/es/diccionario/ingles/signature (observed 2026-11-17).

## C1 provisional: copyright (noun)
- Shortlist global zero-based index 807; original source index 112. Current English definition: legal control over use/copying of creative work; Myanmar `မူပိုင်ခွင့်`; example: “The publisher owns the copyright to the illustrations.”
- Source: Cambridge English Dictionary, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/copyright (observed 2026-11-17), identifies the legal-right noun, alongside a *distinct verb*. Cambridge Learner's Dictionary https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/copyright (observed 2026-11-17) also supports the legal-right noun definition. Neither cited page gives an explicit C1 label for this noun sense. An Oxford result https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/copyright_2 (observed 2026-11-17) labels **copyright adjective**, topic Law and justice, C1; this is **not evidence** that the shortlisted noun sense is C1.
- Finding: the noun's semantics are supported, but exact-sense C1 classification is **not established** by the collected references. Do not transfer a CEFR label from adjective to noun.
- Suggested precise English: “The legal right to control copying, sharing or other permitted uses of an original creative work.” Myanmar: `စာပေ၊ သီချင်း၊ ရုပ်ပုံစသည့် မူရင်းဖန်တီးမှုများကို မိတ္တူကူးခြင်းနှင့် အသုံးပြုခြင်းတို့အား ဥပဒေအရ ထိန်းချုပ်ခွင့်ရှိသည့် မူပိုင်ခွင့်` (editorial proposal, pending fluent Myanmar review). Original example proposal: “The photographer owns the copyright to these images, so we must ask before reusing them.”
- Sense trap: `copyrighted` is an inflected verb/adjectival form, not automatically evidence for the noun's CEFR classification.
