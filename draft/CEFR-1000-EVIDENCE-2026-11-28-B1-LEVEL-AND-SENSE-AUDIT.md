## Exact-sense findings

### 1. direction — CEFR1000-B1-0401 (source index 10)

- **Selected sense:** noun, the way in which someone or something is moving or facing.
- **Source evidence:** Cambridge Learner's Dictionary labels the WAY noun sense **B1**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/direction
- **Level finding:** B1 matches the selected noun sense. Keep it distinct from the B2 phrase `in someone's direction`, and from the broader development, control and purpose senses.
- **Myanmar:** `ဦးတည်ရာ၊ ဦးတည်ချက်` is accurate. In a route question, `ဘယ်ဘက်သို့ သွားရမည့် လမ်းကြောင်း` may be more concrete.
- **Existing example:** “The driver asked which direction to take.” Grammatically sound and sense-aligned.

### 2. independent — CEFR1000-B1-0402 (source index 11)

- **Shortlist definition and example:** “Not controlled by another person or organisation”; “The team made an independent decision.”
- **Source evidence:** Cambridge labels the self-reliance / not needing help sense **B1**, but the not controlled or ruled sense **B2**, and the not influenced sense **B2**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/independent
- **Level finding:** the current definition and example illustrate B2, not the B1 self-reliance sense. This is a direct sense/level mismatch.
- **B1 definition proposal:** “Able to do things without needing help from other people.”
- **Myanmar refinement:** `ကိုယ့်အားကိုယ်ကိုးနိုင်သော၊ အခြားသူ၏အကူအညီ မလိုအပ်သော` expresses self-reliance more clearly than the broader `လွတ်လပ်သော`.
- **Original B1 example proposal:** “After moving into her own flat, she became more independent.”
- **Editorial action:** if retaining B1, change both definition and example to the self-reliance sense; otherwise re-level or replace. Do not treat political independence or an independent expert/study as the B1 sense.

### 3. weekend — CEFR1000-B1-0403 (source index 12)

- **Selected sense:** Saturday and Sunday, when many people do not work.
- **Source evidence:** Cambridge Learner's Dictionary labels this noun sense **A1**, not B1: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/weekend
- **Level finding:** direct A1-versus-B1 conflict. The current example uses the natural British expression `at the weekend`, but grammatical naturalness does not substantiate B1.
- **Myanmar:** `သီတင်းပတ်ကုန်ရက်များ` is suitable.
- **Editorial action:** consider re-level or replace after independent review; do not change the provisional level automatically.

### 4. wonder — CEFR1000-B1-0404 (source index 13)

- **Selected sense:** verb, to ask yourself questions because you want to know something.
- **Source evidence:** Cambridge labels the QUESTION verb sense **B1**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/wonder
- **Level finding:** B1 matches the selected sense. Keep the question/curiosity use separate from the noun `a wonder` and other idiomatic uses.
- **Myanmar:** `သိချင်မိသည်၊ တွေးမိသည်` is natural in this context.
- **Existing example:** “I wonder why the shop is closed today.” Correct embedded wh-clause; no question mark is needed because the whole sentence is a statement.

### 5. cash — CEFR1000-B1-0405 (source index 14)

- **Selected shortlist sense:** money in the form of notes and coins.
- **Source evidence:** Cambridge labels physical notes/coins **A2**, while the broader “money in any form” noun sense is **B1**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/cash
- **Level finding:** the current definition and “The small shop accepts cash only” example point to the A2 physical-payment sense, not clearly to the broader B1 money sense.
- **Myanmar:** `ငွေသား` is natural for notes and coins. For the broader liquidity sense, a reviewer should consider `အသုံးပြုနိုင်သော ငွေကြေး` or another contextually natural wording rather than assuming the two senses are interchangeable.
- **Original B1 example proposal:** “The company is short of cash after a quiet month.”
- **Editorial action:** retain B1 only if the definition, Myanmar meaning and example are revised to the broader money/liquidity sense; otherwise re-level or replace.

### 6. clearly — CEFR1000-B1-0406 (source index 15)

- **Shortlist definition and example:** “In a way that is easy to see, hear or understand”; “Please speak clearly during the presentation.”
- **Source evidence:** Cambridge labels the easy-to-see/hear/understand manner sense **A2**, while the adverb used to express that something is obvious or certain is **B1**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/clearly
- **Level finding:** the current definition and example are A2, not B1.
- **B1 definition proposal:** “Used to show that you think something is obvious or certain.”
- **Myanmar refinement:** `သိသာထင်ရှားသည်မှာ၊ ရှင်းနေသည်မှာ` is closer to the B1 stance/discourse use than the manner meaning `ရှင်းလင်းစွာ`.
- **Original B1 example proposal:** “Clearly, we need to check the figures again.”
- **Editorial action:** revise the sense and example for B1 or re-level/replace; do not conflate the A2 manner adverb with the B1 stance adverb.

### 7. effort — CEFR1000-B1-0407 (source index 17)

- **Selected sense:** noun, the physical or mental energy used to do something.
- **Source evidence:** Cambridge labels both the ATTEMPT and ENERGY noun senses **B1**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/effort
- **Level finding:** B1 matches the selected energy sense.
- **Myanmar:** `ကြိုးစားအားထုတ်မှု` is accurate and natural.
- **Existing example:** “Learning a language requires regular effort.” Grammatically sound; `effort` is correctly uncountable here.

### 8. imagine — CEFR1000-B1-0408 (source index 18)

- **Selected sense:** verb, to create an idea or picture in your mind.
- **Source evidence:** Cambridge labels the CREATE and NOT REAL senses **B1**, while the BELIEVE / probable assumption sense is **B2**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/imagine
- **Level finding:** B1 matches the selected mental-picture sense. Keep it distinct from “I imagine he is busy,” which expresses a B2-type belief or supposition.
- **Myanmar:** `စိတ်ကူးကြည့်သည်` is suitable.
- **Existing example:** “Can you imagine living beside the sea?” Correctly uses `imagine + -ing`; it clearly illustrates forming a mental picture.

### 9. impact — CEFR1000-B1-0409 (source index 19)

- **Selected sense:** noun, a strong effect on a situation.
- **Source evidence:** Cambridge labels the EFFECT noun sense **B2**; the physical-force sense is separately labelled C2 on the inspected general entry: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/impact
- **Level finding:** the current definition and policy/business example describe the B2 effect sense, not B1. No B1 sense was established by the inspected source.
- **Myanmar:** `သက်ရောက်မှု` is accurate for the effect noun.
- **Editorial action:** direct level conflict; re-level or replace after independent review. Do not use the C2 physical-force sense to justify B1.

### 10. civil — CEFR1000-B1-0411 (source index 51)

- **Selected sense:** adjective, connected with ordinary citizens rather than the military.
- **Source evidence:** the Cambridge Learner's Dictionary entry confirms the PEOPLE sense but shows no CEFR label in the indexed extract: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/civil . Cambridge's general English Dictionary labels the closely matching ORDINARY / not-military sense **C1**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/civil
- **Level finding:** B1 is not substantiated; the general Cambridge C1 label is a significant higher-level warning for the selected sense. The fixed phrase `civil rights` in the current example also needs separate treatment from the adjective's broad gloss.
- **Myanmar:** `အရပ်ဘက်ဆိုင်ရာ` works for the civil-versus-military contrast. In `civil rights`, `နိုင်ငံသားအခွင့်အရေး` is more idiomatic and should be reviewed as a phrase.
- **Editorial action:** do not approve B1 from the current evidence. Re-level/redefine or replace only after independent source review.

### 11. multiple — CEFR1000-B1-0418 (source index 88)

- **Selected sense:** adjective, involving more than one person or thing.
- **Source evidence:** the Cambridge Learner's Dictionary entry confirms an adjective sense but the inspected indexed extract does not display an explicit CEFR level: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/multiple
- **Level finding:** B1 remains unsubstantiated by this source. The page's visible gloss “with several parts” is not a clean match for every use of `multiple`; the shortlist's “more than one” definition and website-language example need a source that explicitly supports that exact sense and level.
- **Myanmar:** `တစ်ခုထက်ပိုသော၊ အများအပြားဖြစ်သော` is broadly understandable; the most natural choice depends on the noun modified.
- **Existing example:** “The website supports multiple languages.” Grammatically correct, but it does not establish a B1 label.

### 12. operation — CEFR1000-B1-0419 (source index 89)

- **Selected sense:** noun, an activity or set of activities involved in running something.
- **Source evidence:** Cambridge labels the MEDICAL TREATMENT noun sense **B1**, but the inspected Learner's Dictionary extract does not attach that level to the ORGANIZATION or ACTIVITY senses: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/operation
- **Level finding:** do not transfer the B1 label from the medical sense to the warehouse/operational-activity sense. The current example “The warehouse operation begins at eight o'clock” illustrates the activity sense, whose CEFR level remains unsubstantiated here.
- **Myanmar:** `လုပ်ငန်းဆောင်ရွက်မှု` is suitable for the selected activity sense; `ခွဲစိတ်ကုသမှု` would be the separate medical sense.
- **Editorial action:** obtain exact-sense CEFR evidence or change to a genuinely supported sense; do not infer B1 from the medical entry.

### 13. organization — CEFR1000-B1-0420 (source index 94)

- **Selected sense:** noun, an official group of people working together for a shared purpose.
- **Source evidence:** Cambridge Learner's Dictionary labels the GROUP noun sense **B1**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/organization
- **Level finding:** B1 matches the selected group sense. The same page also shows B1 for planning an activity; its arrangement sense should not be conflated with the group.
- **Myanmar:** `အဖွဲ့အစည်း` is accurate.
- **Existing example:** “The organization provides free classes for adults.” Natural and sense-aligned.
- **British English note:** Cambridge marks `organization` as also having the UK `-isation` spelling. Consider whether the published headword should use British `organisation`; do not silently change the canonical headword or task mapping.

### 14. protect — CEFR1000-B1-0421 (source index 95)

- **Selected sense:** verb, to keep someone or something safe from harm or damage.
- **Source evidence:** Cambridge Learner's Dictionary labels the verb sense **B1**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/protect
- **Level finding:** B1 matches the selected verb sense. Keep the noun `protection` separate; Cambridge labels that noun B2.
- **Myanmar:** `ကာကွယ်သည်` is accurate.
- **Existing example:** “Wear gloves to protect your hands from sharp edges.” Grammatically sound and sense-aligned.

### 15. secret — CEFR1000-B1-0422 (source index 96)

- **Selected sense:** noun, information deliberately kept from other people.
- **Source evidence:** Cambridge labels the noun sense **B1**; the adjective meaning not allowed to be known is also B1: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/secret
- **Level finding:** B1 matches the selected noun sense. Keep the noun `a secret` distinct from adjective `secret` and adverb `secretly`.
- **Myanmar:** `လျှို့ဝှက်ချက်` is accurate.
- **Existing example:** “They kept the birthday surprise a secret.” Correct `keep + object + complement` construction and clear noun use.
