## Findings

### 1. book — CEFR1000-A1-0030 (source index 29)

- **Shortlist sense:** noun; a set of pages with words or pictures bound together; Myanmar `စာအုပ်`; example: “This book is interesting.”
- **Source evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the “TEXT” noun sense A1 and defines it as pages fastened together in a cover for people to read. It separately presents a writing-book sense without the same A1 label in the inspected extract. The selected example clearly points to the reading/text sense.
- **Assessment:** A1 is supported for the selected text noun. The first-pass definition is broadly accurate but could more clearly identify reading rather than accidentally include a notebook.
- **Suggested definition:** “A set of pages fastened together inside a cover for people to read.”
- **Myanmar note:** `စာအုပ်` is suitable for the selected sense.
- **Original example option:** “I left my book on the desk.”
- **Source URL:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/book

### 2. place — CEFR1000-A1-0063 (source index 63)

- **Shortlist sense:** noun; a particular location or area; Myanmar `နေရာ`; example: “This is a quiet place.”
- **Source evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the “SOMEWHERE” noun sense A1. It distinguishes `someone’s home` (A2), an opportunity to participate (B1), and the verb `place` (B2).
- **Assessment:** The selected A1 location sense matches both the definition and example. The headword is highly polysemous, so the noun/“somewhere” sense must stay explicit.
- **Suggested definition:** “A particular position, building, town or area where someone or something is.”
- **Myanmar note:** `နေရာ` is concise and natural for this sense. Do not use the A2 “my place” = someone’s home sense as the defining meaning.
- **Original example option:** “We found a quiet place to sit.”
- **Source URL:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/place

### 3. time — CEFR1000-A1-0042 (source index 42) — **definition/example sense conflict**

- **Shortlist sense:** noun; current definition: “What clocks measure in hours and minutes.” Current example: “What time is it?”; Myanmar `အချိန်`.
- **Source evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the “PARTICULAR POINT” clock-reading/point-in-the-day sense A1. It labels the duration measured in minutes, hours and days A2, and also labels a period of minutes/hours/years A2.
- **Assessment:** The example selects the A1 clock-time sense, but the current definition describes the A2 duration sense. This is a substantive definition/example mismatch and a likely cross-level conflict, not a harmless wording variation.
- **Suggested A1 definition:** “A particular point in the day or night, especially the hour shown on a clock.”
- **Myanmar note:** For the selected clock-time sense, prefer `နာရီပေါ်တွင် ဖော်ပြသော အချိန်၊ သတ်မှတ်ထားသော အချိန်အမှတ်`. The broader `အချိန်` alone can also denote duration and does not disambiguate the sense.
- **Original example option:** “What time does the shop open?”
- **Alternative if the A2 duration sense is retained:** Keep a duration definition such as “The amount of time measured in minutes, hours or days” and use an example such as “I spend a lot of time reading”; that would require an A2 placement decision by the independent reviewer.
- **Source URL:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/time

### 4. people — CEFR1000-A1-0043 (source index 43)

- **Shortlist sense:** plural noun; more than one person; Myanmar `လူတွေ၊ လူများ`; example: “Many people are here.”
- **Source evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the plural-persons sense A1 and defines it as more than one person. It distinguishes the “the people” political/ordinary-population use and the “race/nation” sense.
- **Assessment:** A1 is supported for the plural-persons sense. The shortlist definition and example align.
- **Suggested definition:** “More than one person.”
- **Myanmar note:** `လူတွေ` is conversational; `လူများ` is more formal/written. The two are register variants, not separate CEFR senses.
- **Original example option:** “Many people take the bus to work.”
- **Source URL:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/people

### 5. house — CEFR1000-A1-0062 (source index 62)

- **Shortlist sense:** noun; a building in which people live; Myanmar `အိမ်`; example: “Our house has two rooms.”
- **Source evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the “BUILDING” noun sense A1 and defines it as a building where people live, usually one family or group. The same entry lists other senses: people living in a house, a place for an activity, a legislative House, theatre audience/area, and verb uses.
- **Assessment:** The selected building sense is A1 and the example matches it. Keep it distinct from `home` (the place where someone lives) and from institutional or activity-place uses of `house`.
- **Suggested definition:** “A building where people live, usually one family or group.”
- **Myanmar note:** `အိမ်` is natural; `လူနေအိမ်` can make the physical-building sense explicit when contrast with `home` is needed.
- **Original example option:** “Their house has a small garden.”
- **Source URL:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/house

### 6. help — CEFR1000-A1-0059 (source index 59)

- **Shortlist sense:** verb; “To make a task easier for someone”; Myanmar `ကူညီသည်`; example: “Can you help me?”
- **Source evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the “GIVE HELP” verb sense A1: making it easier for someone to do something. It separately labels “make something easier or better” B2, `help yourself` and `can’t help doing` B1, and the help noun A2.
- **Assessment:** A1 is supported, but the first-pass definition is close enough to the separate B2 “make something easier or better” wording that it should explicitly foreground helping a person do an action.
- **Suggested definition:** “To make it easier for someone to do something.”
- **Myanmar note:** `တစ်စုံတစ်ယောက်အတွက် တစ်ခုခုလုပ်ဆောင်ရ လွယ်ကူအောင် ကူညီပေးသည်` is more sense-specific than `ကူညီသည်` alone.
- **Original example option:** “My sister helps me with my homework.”
- **Source URL:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/help

### 7. see — CEFR1000-A1-0046 (source index 46)

- **Shortlist sense:** verb; noticing something with the eyes; Myanmar currently `မြင်သည်၊ တွေ့သည်`; example: “I can see the moon.”
- **Source evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the “EYES” sense A1 and the “MEET” sense A1; “WATCH” is A2; “UNDERSTAND” and “INFORMATION” are B1; “IMAGINE” is B2.
- **Assessment:** The selected visual sense is A1 and the example is well aligned. The current Myanmar pair `မြင်သည်၊ တွေ့သည်` risks merging visual perception with the separate meet/visit sense.
- **Suggested definition:** “To notice people or things with your eyes.”
- **Myanmar note:** For this exact sense, prefer `မျက်စိဖြင့် မြင်သည်`. Reserve `တွေ့သည်` for the separate meet/encounter use where context supports it.
- **Original example option:** “I can see the lights from my window.”
- **Source URL:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/see

### 8. want — CEFR1000-A1-0048 (source index 48)

- **Shortlist sense:** verb; wishing to have or do something; Myanmar `လိုချင်သည်၊ ဆန္ဒရှိသည်`; example: “I want some water.”
- **Source evidence:** Cambridge Dictionary labels the wish/desire verb sense A1 and explicitly notes that `want` is not used in polite requests. Its British entry gives /wɒnt/.
- **Assessment:** A1 is supported for the wish/desire verb. The example is grammatical and sense-matched, but it is direct rather than a polite request.
- **Suggested definition:** “To wish to have something or to do something.”
- **Myanmar note:** `လိုချင်သည်` is the most direct everyday equivalent; `ဆန္ဒရှိသည်` is more formal. A learner note should contrast direct `I want …` with the more polite `I’d like …`, without implying that the shortlist example is ungrammatical.
- **Original example option:** “I want to learn another language.”
- **Source URL:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/want

### 9. know — CEFR1000-A1-0045 (source index 45)

- **Shortlist sense:** verb; having information or understanding; Myanmar `သိသည်`; example: “I know the answer.”
- **Source evidence:** Cambridge Essential British English labels “have information about something in your mind” A1. It separately labels being certain, knowing how to do something and `let someone know` A2, and familiarity with a person/place B1.
- **Assessment:** The selected “have information” sense is supported at A1 by Cambridge Essential British English. The source is not the Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary entry, so this source-type distinction should remain visible. “Understanding” in the first-pass definition is broad and can blur other uses.
- **Suggested definition:** “To have information about something in your mind.”
- **Myanmar note:** `သိသည်၊ အချက်အလက်တစ်ခုကို သိရှိထားသည်` fits the information sense. Keep “know how to” and familiarity with a person/place separate.
- **Original example option:** “I know her phone number.”
- **Source URL:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/essential-british-english/know

### 10. go — CEFR1000-A1-0049 (source index 49)

- **Shortlist sense:** verb; moving or travelling from one place to another; Myanmar `သွားသည်`; example: “We go to the park.”
- **Source evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the “MOVE” verb sense A1 and also gives an A1 “DO SOMETHING” destination construction. Other uses include B1 disappear/develop/match and B2 machine-operation/time-passing senses.
- **Assessment:** A1 is supported for the movement sense; the definition and example align. Because `go` is highly polysemous, do not use a CEFR label for the headword as if it covered every phrasal, idiomatic or abstract use.
- **Suggested definition:** “To move or travel somewhere.”
- **Myanmar note:** `သွားသည်` is suitable for the selected movement sense.
- **Original example option:** “We go to the market on Saturdays.”
- **Source URL:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/go

### 11. day — CEFR1000-A1-0052 (source index 52)

- **Shortlist sense:** noun; a period of twenty-four hours; Myanmar `နေ့၊ တစ်နေ့တာ`; example: “Today is a busy day.”
- **Source evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the “24 HOURS” noun sense A1. It labels daylight hours, usual work/school hours and the past-time phrase `one day` A2 in the inspected entry.
- **Assessment:** A1 is supported for the 24-hour sense. “Today is a busy day” is grammatical but can invite the A2 work/school-day interpretation.
- **Suggested definition:** “A period of 24 hours.”
- **Myanmar note:** `၂၄ နာရီကြာသော တစ်ရက်တာ` makes the A1 duration boundary explicit; `နေ့ခင်း` is not the same sense.
- **Original example option:** “A week has seven days.”
- **Source URL:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/day

### 12. work — CEFR1000-A1-0053 (source index 53)

- **Shortlist sense:** verb; current definition: “To do a job or activity that needs effort”; Myanmar `အလုပ်လုပ်သည်`; example: “I work in a shop.”
- **Source evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the “JOB” verb sense A1: doing a job, especially one done to earn money. It labels a machine working A2, something being effective/successful B1, and the noun “effort” sense B1; the noun “job” sense is A1.
- **Assessment:** The example selects the A1 job verb, but the current definition adds “activity that needs effort”, which is broader and risks importing the B1 effort sense or other non-job uses.
- **Suggested definition:** “To do a job, especially one you do to earn money.”
- **Myanmar note:** `အလုပ်အကိုင်တစ်ခုကို လုပ်ကိုင်သည်` is more exact for this A1 verb than a broad effort/activity paraphrase.
- **Original example option:** “My uncle works in a small hotel.”
- **Source URL:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/work

### 13. old — CEFR1000-A1-0060 (source index 60)

- **Shortlist sense:** adjective; having lived or existed for many years; Myanmar `အသက်ကြီးသော၊ ဟောင်းသော`; example: “My grandfather is old.”
- **Source evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the “LIVED LONG” adjective sense A1 and the age-question use A1. It separately labels “USED A LOT”, “old friend/enemy” and “BEFORE/former” uses A2.
- **Assessment:** A1 is supported for the selected age/long-existence adjective. The example is specifically about a person; the paired Myanmar gloss should not imply that every A2 “old friend” or “old clothes” use has the same sense.
- **Suggested definition:** “Having lived or existed for a long time.”
- **Myanmar note:** For the example about a person, `အသက်ကြီးသော` is exact. `ဟောင်းသော` can describe something that has existed for a long time, but “used/owned for a long time” and “old friend” are separately labelled A2 uses.
- **Original example option:** “My grandfather is old, but he still walks every morning.”
- **Source URL:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/old

### 14. little — CEFR1000-A1-0061 (source index 61)

- **Shortlist sense:** adjective; small in size or amount; Myanmar `သေးငယ်သော၊ နည်းသော`; example: “I have a little dog.”
- **Source evidence:** Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary labels the adjective “SMALL” and “YOUNG” senses A1. It separately labels the quantifier meaning “not much or not enough” B1, “a little” as a small amount B1, and “short in time or distance” B2.
- **Assessment:** The example is the A1 small/young adjective. The current Myanmar pair `သေးငယ်သော၊ နည်းသော` risks merging the adjective with the separate quantifier; “နည်းသော” is not the best gloss for a small dog.
- **Suggested definition:** “Small in size.”
- **Myanmar note:** For `a little dog`, prefer `ခွေးသေးသေးလေး` or `ခွေးလေး`. Keep `နည်းသော` for quantity/amount contexts and explain the B1 quantifier separately.
- **Original example option:** “A little bird sat on the wall.”
- **Source URL:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/little
