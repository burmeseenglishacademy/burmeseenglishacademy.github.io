## Current provisional content and editorial issues

- `a`: “Used before one person or thing when it is not specified.” Myanmar: “တစ်ခု၊ တစ်ယောက် (မသတ်မှတ်ရသေးသော)”; example: “I have a book.”
- `an`: “Used before one person or thing when the next word starts with a vowel sound.” Myanmar: “တစ်ခု၊ တစ်ယောက် (သရသံဖြင့်စသော စကားလုံးရှေ့တွင်)”; example: “She has an apple.”

Both original examples are grammatical. However, the `a` definition omits the phonological choice, and both omit the **singular countable noun phrase** constraint. The `an` wording “next word” is useful: adjectives and abbreviations can determine the immediate sound, even if the noun itself begins differently. Do not teach the misleading rule “an before vowel letters.” Neither article means the number one in every context; Myanmar “တစ်” is a pedagogical gloss, not an obligatory word-for-word translation.
