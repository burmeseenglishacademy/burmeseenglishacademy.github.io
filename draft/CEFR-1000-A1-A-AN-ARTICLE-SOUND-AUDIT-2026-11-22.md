## Source observations, not CEFR signoff
1. Cambridge Grammar Today, *A/an and the*, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/a-an (observed 2026-11-22): indefinite articles refer to non-specific people/things; `a` precedes a **consonant sound**, `an` a **vowel sound**. The following *spoken sound*, not the written first letter, controls selection. Cambridge illustrates `a university`, `a one-year-old child`, `an hour`, and `an MP3 player`.
2. British Council LearnEnglish, *The indefinite article: a and an*, https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/indefinite-article (observed 2026-11-22): labels this as **beginner** grammar, explains singular count nouns and non-specific reference, excludes plural and uncountable nouns, and confirms sound-based selection. **Beginner is not a source-provided exact A1 lexical-sense certification.** Independent reviewers must secure appropriate explicit CEFR evidence if claiming A1.

## Editorial problems requiring adjudication
- The `a` definition omits its consonant-sound condition and the singular countable noun constraint; paired entries are asymmetrical.
- Both Burmese glosses may misleadingly imply that `a/an` always translate as standalone “တစ်ခု/တစ်ယောက်”. In natural Myanmar, an indefinite article often has **no direct word-for-word equivalent**. Explain the grammatical function rather than forcing a numeral into every translation.
- `an` is chosen by the **immediately following spoken sound**, including an adjective (`an old book` but `a useful book`), not necessarily the noun's first letter.
- Do not equate `a/an` with `one` in every context, and do not put them directly before uncountable nouns (`an information`) or plural nouns (`a books`).
- Keep `a` and `an` as distinct displayed headwords if required by the user-approved 200-per-level inventory, but have a reviewer assess whether treating two allomorphs of one indefinite article as two independent lexical **senses** meets the final deduplication policy. Never silently collapse either entry and leave A1 below 200.
