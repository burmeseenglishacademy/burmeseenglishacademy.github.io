1. Cambridge Learner's Dictionary, **he**, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/he (observed 2026-11-08): **A1** subject pronoun referring back to a male person. UK strong /hiː/, weak /hi/.
2. Cambridge Essential British English, **he**, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/essential-british-english/he (observed 2026-11-08): **A1** reference to previously mentioned man or male animal.
3. Cambridge Learner's Dictionary, **she**, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/she (observed 2026-11-08): **A1** subject pronoun referring back to a female person. UK strong /ʃiː/, weak /ʃi/.
4. Cambridge Essential British English, **she**, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/essential-british-english/she (observed 2026-11-08): **A1** reference to previously mentioned woman.

**Scope:** Cambridge's A1 marking supports these *subject-pronoun/anaphoric* uses, not all meanings of `he` or `she`. Do not include the noun uses, old-fashioned generic masculine `he`, or metaphorical `she` for vehicles/countries as if these were the target A1 sense. Do not infer a CEFR grade for such other senses.

## Editorial observations and proposed teaching text (not yet applied)
- Existing `he` definition: “A male person who has already been mentioned.” This describes a person rather than the **pronoun**; improve to “Used as the subject of a verb to refer to a man or boy already mentioned.”
- Existing `she` definition has the same part-of-speech issue; improve to “Used as the subject of a verb to refer to a woman or girl already mentioned.”
- Proposed Myanmar `he`: “အရင်က ပြောထားပြီးသော အမျိုးသား သို့မဟုတ် ယောက်ျားလေးကို ပြန်လည်ရည်ညွှန်းသည့် ကတ္တားနာမ်စား။” Teaching gloss: “သူ (အမျိုးသား)”.
- Proposed Myanmar `she`: “အရင်က ပြောထားပြီးသော အမျိုးသမီး သို့မဟုတ် မိန်းကလေးကို ပြန်လည်ရည်ညွှန်းသည့် ကတ္တားနာမ်စား။” Teaching gloss: “သူ (အမျိုးသမီး)”.
- Current inventory examples “He is my brother.” and “She is my sister.” are grammatical, but lack an **explicit earlier referent**. For exact-sense instruction, proposed original examples:
  - “This is Ko Min. He is my brother.” — “ဒါ ကိုမင်းပါ။ သူက ကျွန်တော့်အစ်ကိုပါ။”
  - “This is May. She is my sister.” — “ဒါ မေပါ။ သူက ကျွန်မရဲ့ ညီမပါ။”
- Myanmar kinship terms depend on speaker and sibling age; **independent fluent Myanmar reviewer must confirm** whether examples should instead use neutral “မောင်နှမ” constructions or specify speaker/age. The examples above are provisional and not linguistically signed off.
- Subject/object contrast: `He knows her` vs `She knows him`; do not use `him` or `her` as the subject. British George/Emma audio must verify strong/weak forms in connected speech through actual listening, not merely file presence.
