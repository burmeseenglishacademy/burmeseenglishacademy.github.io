## 1. signature — probable level-placement conflict

- Shortlist index **803**, original source index **75**, provisional **C1**, noun.
- Existing English: “A person's name written in their own handwriting.”
- Existing Myanmar: “လက်မှတ်ရေးထိုးချက်”.
- Existing example: “Please add your signature at the bottom of the form.”
- **Direct CEFR evidence:** Cambridge English Dictionary, *signature*, noun **NAME**, countable, **B1**, specifically the person's own written name used to indicate authorship or agreement: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/signature?topic=signing-and-signatures
- **Independent dictionary-family corroboration, not an independent editorial review:** Cambridge Learner's Dictionary also marks the handwritten-name noun **B1**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/signature
- **Sense match:** existing definition and example concern handwritten name, not an advanced figurative “signature style” or adjective. The provisional C1 assignment is therefore unsupported by these two explicit B1 labels. Requires independent level adjudication and replacement/rebalancing if reassigned.
- **Myanmar editorial note:** “လက်မှတ်ရေးထိုးချက်” can sound like the *act* of signing, while the example requires the resulting written signature. Proposed, unapproved alternative: “လက်မှတ်၊ မိမိလက်ရေးဖြင့် ရေးထိုးထားသော အမည်”.
- **Original candidate example, not independently checked:** “The receptionist asked me to sign my name because my signature was missing.” The current example is grammatical and may be retained after independent review.
- **Do not** promote, relevel, or count as verified on the basis of this observation.

## 2. trigger — C1 sense corroboration

- Shortlist index **801**, original source index **32**, provisional **C1**, verb.
- Existing English: “To cause a particular event or reaction to happen.”
- Existing Myanmar: “ဖြစ်ပေါ်စေသည်၊ အစပျိုးစေသည်”.
- Existing example: “A sudden price rise could trigger more complaints.”
- **Direct CEFR evidence:** Cambridge English Dictionary, *trigger*, transitive verb, **C1**, “to cause something to start”: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/trigger
- **Sense corroboration:** Collins English Dictionary, verb meaning cause an event/situation to begin: https://www.collinsdictionary.com/dictionary/english/trigger . Collins corroborates the sense, not necessarily the CEFR level.
- **Sense match:** the shortlist example has a causal event-starting verb, not the firearm-part noun or trauma-specific use. The Cambridge C1 label supports this *specific* sense, but does not constitute independent bilingual review.
- **Myanmar editorial note:** current meaning is intelligible; to distinguish causing an onset from generic causation, consider “ဖြစ်ပေါ်လာစေသည်၊ အစပျိုးဖြစ်ပေါ်စေသည်” (proposed only).
- **Original candidate example, not independently checked:** “The unexpected announcement triggered a wave of questions from staff.”
