## Publisher-level caution

Oxford states that CEFR describes language ability and does not itself grade individual vocabulary items; Oxford aligns its own core word lists to CEFR as a pedagogical guide. Oxford list labels are therefore publisher-specific word/POS evidence, not Council of Europe assignments and not necessarily exact-sense labels. Cambridge Learner's Dictionary sometimes labels individual senses and patterns. Conflicting publisher labels must be retained as a conflict for editorial adjudication, not averaged or silently resolved.

Sources:
- Oxford explanation: https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/about/wordlists/cefr

### A2 — support (verb), task CEFR1000-A2-0219, source index 198

- **Shortlist:** “To help someone succeed or manage a difficulty.” Myanmar: `ကူညီပံ့ပိုးသည်`. Example: “My colleagues support me when I need help.”
- **Source observation:** Oxford's 3000/5000 list shows `support verb` at A2 (word/POS-level alignment). Cambridge Learner's Dictionary has separate verb senses: agreement (B2), financial support (B1), and holding weight (B2); its “prove” sense has no displayed CEFR label. Its noun sense meaning help/encouragement is B1. The Cambridge page does not provide an explicit A2 label for the exact “help someone succeed/manage a difficulty” verb sense.
- **Source URLs:** https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/wordlists/oxford3000-5000?dataset=english&level=b2&list=ox5000 ; https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/support
- **Disposition:** A2 is supported by Oxford at word/POS level, but exact verb-sense evidence is incomplete. Do not treat the B1 noun sense as proof that the verb is B1, or the Oxford word label as a sense-specific approval.
- **Myanmar/example note:** The gloss is understandable but formal. A more conversational option is `လုပ်ဖော်ကိုင်ဖက်တွေက အကူအညီလိုတဲ့အခါ ကျွန်တော့်ကို ကူညီပံ့ပိုးပေးတယ်။` The English example is grammatical and natural. Translation and register still require independent Myanmar review.

### A2 — available (adjective), task CEFR1000-A2-0298, source index 585

- **Shortlist:** “Able to be used, bought, or reached at a particular time.” Myanmar: `ရရှိနိုင်သော၊ အသုံးပြုနိုင်သော`. Example: “The meeting room is available after lunch.”
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Cambridge Learner's Dictionary labels A2 both “ready to use/get” and “not busy/able to do something”. The meeting-room example most naturally selects the “not busy / free for use” sense.
- **Source:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/available
- **Editorial note:** The current definition combines several senses and the word “reached” is not needed for this example. A tighter proposal is “Able to be used or obtained at a particular time.” Myanmar for the example could be `နေ့လယ်စာစားပြီးနောက် အစည်းအဝေးခန်းမကို အသုံးပြုလို့ရပါတယ်။` The original sentence is grammatical and natural; its predicative pattern is `be available`.

### B1 — avoid (verb), task CEFR1000-B1-0469, source index 309

- **Shortlist:** “To keep away from something or prevent it from happening.” Myanmar: `ရှောင်ရှားသည်၊ မဖြစ်အောင် ကာကွယ်သည်`. Example: “We leave early to avoid heavy traffic.”
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Cambridge Learner's Dictionary labels “stay away from a person, place, situation, etc.” B1, but “prevent something from happening” and `avoid doing something` B2.
- **Source:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/avoid
- **Risk:** The definition combines B1 and B2 senses. “Leave early to avoid heavy traffic” is grammatical, but can be interpreted as preventing/avoiding the traffic condition rather than simply staying away from a place or situation.
- **B1-only example option:** “We avoid that road during rush hour.” Myanmar: `ယာဉ်ကြောပိတ်ဆို့ချိန်မှာ အဲဒီလမ်းကို ကျွန်တော်တို့ ရှောင်တယ်။`
- **Disposition:** Keep the B1 entry on hold until the intended sense is narrowed. Do not let the B2 prevent/do-not-do sense inherit the B1 label.

### B1 — improve (verb), task CEFR1000-B1-0536, source index 611

- **Shortlist:** “To become better or make something better.” Myanmar: `တိုးတက်ကောင်းမွန်စေသည်`. Example: “Regular practice can improve your speaking skills.”
- **Exact-sense challenge:** Cambridge Learner's Dictionary explicitly labels the verb sense “to get better or to make something better” A2. The example uses the transitive “make something better” pattern and aligns with that A2 sense.
- **Source:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/improve
- **Level risk:** The shortlist places this exact everyday sense at B1, one level above Cambridge's explicit A2 label. This is a genuine level challenge, not an independent decision to move the word.
- **Myanmar note:** The current gloss only expresses the causative/transitive reading. To cover both senses, consider `တိုးတက်ကောင်းမွန်လာသည်၊ ပိုကောင်းအောင် လုပ်သည်။`
- **Disposition:** Flag for B1→A2 rebalancing or replacement during independent adjudication. Do not edit the 200-per-level shortlist automatically.

### B1 — achieve (verb), task CEFR1000-B1-0545, source index 620

- **Shortlist:** “To succeed in reaching a goal through effort.” Myanmar: `အောင်မြင်စွာ ရရှိသည်`. Example: “He worked hard to achieve his goal.”
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Cambridge Learner's Dictionary labels B1 the sense “to succeed in doing something good, usually by working hard” and gives examples involving achieving an ambition and a goal. Oxford's 3000/5000 word list labels the verb A2 at word/POS level.
- **Sources:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/achieve ; https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/wordlists/oxford3000-5000?dataset=english&level=c1&list=ox5000
- **Cross-publisher conflict:** Cambridge's exact-sense learner entry is B1; Oxford's word/POS list is A2. Neither label should be silently discarded. Oxford's label is not an exact-sense label.
- **Myanmar note:** `အောင်မြင်စွာ ရရှိသည်` is broad and slightly unnatural for a goal. More precise: `ကြိုးစားအားထုတ်ပြီး ရည်မှန်းချက်တစ်ခုကို အောင်မြင်စွာ ပြည့်မီသည်။` Example translation option: `သူက သူ့ရည်မှန်းချက်ကို အောင်မြင်အောင် ကြိုးစားအားထုတ်ခဲ့တယ်။`
- **Disposition:** Keep pending; independent reviewers must adjudicate the publisher discrepancy and the target learner profile.

### C1 — accommodate (verb), task CEFR1000-C1-0884, source index 650

- **Shortlist:** “To make space for someone or adjust to meet a need.” Myanmar: `နေရာပေးသည်၊ လိုအပ်ချက်နှင့် ကိုက်ညီအောင် စီစဉ်ပေးသည်`. Example: “The hall can accommodate two hundred guests.”
- **Exact-sense issue:** The example selects only the capacity/space sense (“have or provide the space someone or something needs”). Cambridge Business English provides that definition but no CEFR label on the page. Oxford's 3000/5000 list labels `accommodate verb` B2, not C1.
- **Sources:** https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/accommodate ; https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/wordlists/oxford3000-5000?dataset=english&level=c1&list=ox5000
- **Level risk:** The current C1 assignment is not supported by the Oxford list for this verb, and the Cambridge page inspected does not supply a C1 label. The shortlist definition also merges capacity and adjustment/suitability senses.
- **Myanmar note:** For the actual example, the natural meaning is “ဆံ့သည်”: `ခန်းမမှာ ဧည့်သည် ၂၀၀ ဦးအထိ ဆံ့တယ်။` The current two-part gloss obscures this capacity sense.
- **Disposition:** High-priority C1 level/sense challenge. Consider B2 reassignment or a different C1 sense/entry only after independent adjudication and count-preserving rebalancing.
