## 1. world — task CEFR1000-A2-0201
- **Current A2 noun definition:** “The Earth and all the people and places on it.”
- **Current Myanmar:** “ကမ္ဘာကြီး”
- **Current example:** “People around the world speak different languages.”
- **Observed dictionary evidence:** Cambridge Learner's Dictionary explicitly marks **A1** for **the world**, meaning Earth and all the people, places and things on it. The same page distinguishes **B1** for a domain such as the entertainment world and **A2** for “your world” (personal life and experiences). These labels are **sense-specific**, not blanket headword levels.
- Source: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/world (observed 2026-11-22). Supplemental sense distinctions: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/world (observed 2026-11-22; different dictionary sections do not all supply CEFR labels).
- **Finding:** The existing A2 gloss and example appear to express Cambridge's A1 “the world” sense. Candidate level A1, **pending independent adjudication**; do not silently swap a different sense to justify A2.
- **Candidate English definition:** “The Earth, including its people and places.”
- **Candidate Myanmar meaning:** “ကမ္ဘာကြီး၊ ကမ္ဘာပေါ်ရှိ လူများနှင့် နေရာဒေသများ” (the longer rendering is explanatory; reviewer should judge whether concise “ကမ္ဘာကြီး” is more natural).
- **Original candidate example:** “People around the world learn English for different reasons.”
- **Original candidate Myanmar example:** “ကမ္ဘာတစ်ဝန်းက လူတွေဟာ အကြောင်းရင်းအမျိုးမျိုးကြောင့် အင်္ဂလိပ်စာကို သင်ယူကြတယ်။”
- **Risk:** Headword `world` versus the dictionary's explicitly labelled phrase `the world`; verify whether the teaching example genuinely instantiates that construction and record the precise sense and determiner in reviewer evidence.

## 2. life — task CEFR1000-A2-0202
- **Current A2 noun definition:** “A person's experience of being alive.”
- **Current Myanmar:** “ဘဝ”
- **Current example:** “Living in a new city changed her life.”
- **Observed dictionary evidence:** Cambridge Learner's Dictionary explicitly labels **A1** for the time between birth and death, **B1** for a way of living and **B1** for one part of someone's existence; it also distinguishes **B2** for energy/activity and existence of a person. Cambridge Essential British English independently presents A1 for lifespan and B1 for a way of living. Cambridge English Dictionary marks **A1** for time alive and **B1** for way of living. These are dictionary editorial labels, **not independent human signoff on this entry**.
- Sources: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/life ; https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/essential-british-english/life ; https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/life (all observed 2026-11-22).
- **Finding:** The current definition “experience of being alive” blends lifespan/existence and lived experience. The example “changed her life” is not unequivocally the A1 lifespan sense; it can mean her circumstances/way of living (B1) or overall experience. **Do not auto-reclassify**; request exact-sense adjudication and consider B1 if the intended sense is way of living.
- **Candidate B1 sense-specific English definition:** “The way someone lives and the experiences they have.”
- **Candidate Myanmar meaning:** “နေထိုင်မှုဘဝ၊ ဘဝနေထိုင်ပုံ” (review for naturalness and exact scope).
- **Original candidate example:** “Moving to the countryside gave her a quieter life.”
- **Original candidate Myanmar example:** “ကျေးလက်ဒေသကို ပြောင်းရွှေ့ပြီးနောက် သူမရဲ့ဘဝက ပိုပြီး အေးချမ်းလာတယ်။”
- **Alternative A1 sense** (a different editorial decision, not an automatic replacement): “The period between a person's birth and death”; “လူတစ်ယောက် မွေးဖွားချိန်မှ သေဆုံးချိန်အထိရှိသော သက်တမ်း”; example “He lived a long life.” A1 rewording must be reviewed as a **sense change**, not treated as proof that the original A2 example is A1.
- **Grammar:** `life` can be countable or uncountable depending on sense. The article and possessive in examples matter; do not force a single countability label across all senses.
