## C1 task CEFR1000-C1-0802 — trigger (verb)
- Source index: 32.
- Existing definition: “To cause a particular event or reaction to happen.”
- Existing Myanmar: “ဖြစ်ပေါ်စေသည်၊ အစပျိုးစေသည်”
- Existing example: “A sudden price rise could trigger more complaints.”
- **Exact-sense evidence:** Cambridge English Dictionary labels the transitive verb meaning *cause something to start* **C1**, consistent with this provisional level: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/uk/dictionary/english/trigger (English Dictionary, verb section; URL language prefix does not change the English entry). Cambridge Learner's Dictionary https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/trigger distinguishes transitive verb from noun senses, but the displayed learner entry does **not** explicitly print a CEFR level. Therefore do not cite that learner entry alone as C1 evidence.
- **Editorial issue:** “particular event or reaction” is broad and can blur the intended start-of-event meaning with emotional-trigger terminology and the noun *trigger* (gun component or causal event). The price-rise example is grammatical and illustrates an event causing complaints, but should not be confused with a noun sense.
- Candidate definition (author-written): “To cause an event, process or reaction to begin.”
- Candidate Myanmar (author-written): “အဖြစ်အပျက်တစ်ခု၊ လုပ်ငန်းစဉ်တစ်ခု သို့မဟုတ် တုံ့ပြန်မှုတစ်ခု စတင်ဖြစ်ပေါ်လာစေသည်။”
- Candidate original example: “The unexpected announcement triggered a wave of customer enquiries.” (Past-tense transitive verb; *a wave of enquiries* is its object.)
- Independent reviewer must check whether the C1-labelled English Dictionary sense exactly covers the intended example, confirm fluent Myanmar and original example, and distinguish metaphorical reaction from other noun/psychological meanings.

## C1 task CEFR1000-C1-0823 — upgrade (verb)
- Source index: 263.
- Existing definition: “To improve something by replacing it with a newer or better version.”
- Existing Myanmar: “အဆင့်မြှင့်တင်သည်”
- Existing example: “We will upgrade the software next month.”
- **Exact-sense conflict:** Cambridge Learner's Dictionary explicitly labels the transitive *improve to a higher-quality or newer model* sense **B2**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/upgrade . Cambridge English Dictionary also labels the general improve/change-to-newer verb **B2**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/upgrade . This is the very sense and grammatical pattern used in the provisional C1 example; a C1 placement lacks support from these sources.
- Candidate definition (author-written): “To improve equipment or software, often by replacing it with a newer or better version.”
- Candidate Myanmar (author-written): “စက်ပစ္စည်း သို့မဟုတ် ဆော့ဖ်ဝဲကို ပိုမိုကောင်းမွန်သော၊ ပိုမိုခေတ်မီသော ဗားရှင်းဖြင့် အစားထိုးခြင်း သို့မဟုတ် ပြုပြင်မွမ်းမံခြင်းဖြင့် အဆင့်မြှင့်တင်သည်။”
- Candidate original example: “The library plans to upgrade its computers before the new term.” (Transitive verb; *its computers* is the object.)
