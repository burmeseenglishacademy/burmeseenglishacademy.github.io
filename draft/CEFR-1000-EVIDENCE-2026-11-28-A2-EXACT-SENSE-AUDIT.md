## Exact-sense findings

### 1. world — CEFR1000-A2-0201

- **Shortlist sense:** noun, the Earth and all people and places on it.
- **Cambridge evidence:** Cambridge Learner's Dictionary labels this exact core sense **A1**. It separately labels a person's life/experiences use at A2 and a specialised activity/subject-domain use at B1: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/world
- **Level finding:** the current definition and example (“People around the world speak different languages”) fit the A1 Earth/world sense, not an evidenced A2 sense. The A2 placement is a direct level conflict for the selected meaning.
- **Myanmar:** `ကမ္ဘာကြီး` is natural for the core noun. A fuller learner gloss may be `ကမ္ဘာကြီး၊ ကမ္ဘာပေါ်ရှိ လူများနှင့် နေရာများ`.
- **Original A1-aligned example option:** “People from many countries came together to discuss problems facing the world.”
- **Disposition:** keep on hold; independent reviewer should decide whether to re-level to A1 or select a genuinely A2 sense. Do not silently change the level.

### 2. life — CEFR1000-A2-0202

- **Shortlist sense:** “A person's experience of being alive”; example: “Living in a new city changed her life.”
- **Cambridge evidence:** Cambridge Learner's Dictionary labels the period from birth to death **A1**, a way of living **B1**, and a person's existence in another selected use **B2**. No A2 label was visible for the broad sense currently written: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/life
- **Level finding:** the definition is too broad to identify one CEFR sense. The example is closer to a person's way of living/experience than to the clearly labelled A1 time-between-birth-and-death sense. The current A2 assignment is not substantiated by the inspected source.
- **Myanmar:** `ဘဝ` is a useful general gloss but does not disambiguate the senses. For the A1 time sense, `မွေးဖွားချိန်မှ သေဆုံးချိန်အထိ လူတစ်ဦး၏ ဘဝကာလ` is clearer; for the B1 way-of-living sense, `နေထိုင်မှုပုံစံ` may be needed.
- **Original A1-aligned example option:** “He had a happy life with his family.”
- **Disposition:** hold for sense selection and independent CEFR adjudication; do not treat the general gloss as proof of A2.

### 3. through — CEFR1000-A2-0203

- **Selected sense:** preposition, movement from one side/end of a place to the other.
- **Cambridge evidence:** Cambridge Dictionary and Cambridge Essential English Dictionary label this place/movement sense **A2**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/through and https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/essential-british-english/through
- **British pronunciation reference:** /θruː/ (Cambridge UK entry).
- **Level finding:** A2 is supported for the selected place sense. Keep it distinct from “from beginning to end”, “because of”, and “using/with the help of” senses, which Cambridge labels B1 on the Essential page.
- **Myanmar:** the current `တစ်ဖက်မှ တစ်ဖက်သို့ ဖြတ်သန်း၍` is understandable; in a natural sentence, `…ကို ဖြတ်၍` or `…ကို ဖြတ်လျှောက်၍` may read more fluently.
- **Existing example:** “We walked through the park to reach the station.” The preposition and purpose infinitive are grammatical and match the A2 place sense.
- **Original alternative:** “The children ran through the garden after lunch.”
- **Disposition:** exact-sense/level match in author research; still requires independent English, Myanmar, example and CEFR signoff.

### 4. while — CEFR1000-A2-0204

- **Selected sense:** conjunction meaning during the time that another action happens.
- **Cambridge evidence:** Cambridge Learner's Dictionary labels the DURING conjunction sense **A2**. It separately labels the comparing use B1, the although/contrast use B2, and the noun phrase “a while” B1: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/while
- **British pronunciation reference:** /waɪl/.
- **Level finding:** A2 is supported only for the DURING conjunction sense. The entry must not combine the comparison, concession or noun senses.
- **Myanmar:** `နေစဉ်` is the concise conjunction equivalent; avoid translating this selected sense with a contrastive `သော်လည်း`.
- **Existing example:** “I listened to music while I cooked dinner.” Grammatically correct; the two past-tense clauses express overlapping actions.
- **Original alternative:** “Please keep the door closed while the machine is running.”
- **Disposition:** exact-sense/level match in author research; independent signoff remains outstanding.

### 5. part — CEFR1000-A2-0205

- **Shortlist sense:** count noun, one piece or section of a larger whole; example: “The final part of the lesson is a short quiz.”
- **Cambridge evidence:** Cambridge Dictionary labels the countable “separate piece” sense **A1**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/part
- **Level finding:** the definition and example both point to the countable separate-piece/section sense, which is explicitly A1 on the inspected page. The A2 placement is therefore not supported for the selected sense. Cambridge also presents other uses of `part`; they must not be blended into this entry without a separate exact-sense decision.
- **Myanmar:** `အစိတ်အပိုင်း` is natural for the selected countable sense.
- **Original A1-aligned example option:** “One part of the machine needs to be replaced.”
- **Disposition:** hold for independent re-level/replacement decision; do not change the A2 count in the provisional manifest.

### 6. team — CEFR1000-A2-0207

- **Shortlist sense:** a group of people who work or play together; example: “Our team meets every Saturday.”
- **Cambridge evidence:** Cambridge Learner's Dictionary labels the sports-team sense **A2** and the work-group sense (for example, a management team) **B1**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/team
- **British pronunciation reference:** /tiːm/.
- **Level finding:** the definition merges two senses with different CEFR labels. The existing meeting example is ambiguous and can readily be read as a work group (B1); it does not secure the A2 sports sense.
- **Myanmar:** `အသင်း၊ အဖွဲ့` is broad. For the A2 sports sense, `အားကစားတစ်မျိုးကို အတူယှဉ်ပြိုင်ကစားသော အသင်း` is more precise.
- **Original A2 example option:** “Our school team won the final match.”
- **Suggested translation:** `ကျောင်းအားကစားအသင်းက နောက်ဆုံးပွဲစဉ်မှာ အနိုင်ရခဲ့သည်။`
- **Disposition:** hold until the reviewer selects one sense and aligns the definition, Myanmar gloss and example. If A2 is retained, use the sports sense only.

### 7. someone — CEFR1000-A2-0208

- **Selected sense:** indefinite pronoun for a person whose identity is unknown or not stated.
- **Cambridge evidence:** Cambridge Learner's Dictionary labels this pronoun sense **A2** and notes that it normally takes a singular verb: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/someone
- **British pronunciation reference:** /ˈsʌm.wʌn/.
- **Level finding:** the selected pronoun sense and A2 label match. Keep the entry separate from compounds/phrases such as `someone else` and from any unrelated named-entity use.
- **Myanmar:** `တစ်စုံတစ်ယောက်` is concise and natural.
- **Existing example:** “Someone left an umbrella on the bus.” Correct singular agreement; the identity is unspecified.
- **Original alternative:** “Someone has left a notebook in the meeting room.”
- **Disposition:** exact-sense/level match in author research; independent bilingual and example review remains required.

### 8. must — CEFR1000-A2-0209

- **Selected sense:** modal verb expressing necessity or obligation; example: “You must wear a helmet at work.”
- **Cambridge evidence:** Cambridge English Grammar Today explains modal form, base-verb complementation, obligation, prohibition and the distinction between `must` and `have to`, but the inspected grammar pages do **not** assign a CEFR level to this lexical use: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/must and https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/have-to-and-must
- **Level finding:** the grammar evidence supports the form and obligation meaning, not the shortlist's A2 assignment. Do not convert a grammar-topic level or a third-party study-guide label into an exact-sense CEFR claim.
- **Myanmar:** `မဖြစ်မနေ … ရမည် / … ရတယ်` can convey obligation; register and force depend on context.
- **Example caution:** the workplace helmet sentence is grammatical, but an externally imposed workplace rule may naturally take `have to` in some contexts. Cambridge explains that `must` often expresses an obligation felt or imposed by the speaker, while `have to` commonly expresses an external requirement.
- **Original alternative:** “You must stop when the traffic light is red.”
- **Suggested translation:** `မီးပွိုင့် အနီပြနေချိန်မှာ မဖြစ်မနေ ရပ်ရမယ်။`
- **Disposition:** A2 remains unsubstantiated by the inspected CEFR-labelled sources; hold for independent level evidence and a context-sensitive Myanmar review.

### 9. company — CEFR1000-A2-0211

- **Selected sense:** count noun, an organisation that sells goods or services.
- **Cambridge evidence:** Cambridge Essential English Dictionary labels the business-organisation sense **A2**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/essential-british-english/company
- **British pronunciation reference:** /ˈkʌm.pə.ni/.
- **Level finding:** the A2 business-organisation sense matches the shortlist definition and example. Keep it separate from the uncountable companionship sense (for example, “enjoy someone's company”) and the visitors-to-a-home use shown on the same page.
- **Myanmar:** `ကုမ္ပဏီ၊ စီးပွားရေးလုပ်ငန်း` is suitable for the selected business sense; `အဖော်` belongs to the companionship sense and should not be merged into this gloss.
- **Existing example:** “She works for a small company.” Natural and sense-aligned.
- **Original alternative:** “The company opened a second office near the port.”
- **Disposition:** exact-sense/level match in author research; independent signoff remains outstanding.
