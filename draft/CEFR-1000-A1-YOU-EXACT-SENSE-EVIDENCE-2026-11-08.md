## Exact sense and Myanmar editorial proposal
- Retain only **A1 second-person addressee pronoun**: the person or people directly spoken/written to. Do not merge the **A2 generic-you** meaning (“people in general”) into the A1 teaching definition. A shared spelling does not license an unverified cross-level sense.
- Proposed original English gloss: “The pronoun used for the person or people you are speaking or writing to.”
- Proposed fluent Myanmar explanation: “ကိုယ်က တိုက်ရိုက်စကားပြောနေသူ၊ စာရေးဆက်သွယ်နေသူ တစ်ဦး သို့မဟုတ် အများကို ရည်ညွှန်းသော နာမ်စား။”
- Myanmar learner-facing options depend on age, social distance and register: `မင်း` (familiar), `ရှင်` (some polite speech contexts), `ခင်ဗျား` (some polite speech contexts), `သင်` (formal/written). Avoid implying that all choices are interchangeable in all situations.
- Original subject example: **“You are early today.”** → **“ဒီနေ့ မင်း စောစောရောက်လာတာပဲ။”** (familiar register). Original object example: **“I can help you.”** → **“ငါ မင်းကို ကူညီပေးနိုင်တယ်။”** (familiar register). Both refer to a directly addressed listener, not generic people. Check grammar, register and example originality independently before adopting.
- Contrast requiring separate sense review: **“You never know what will happen.”** is potentially generic `you` and must not be used as proof of the A1 addressee sense.
