## Exact-sense findings

### 1. order — CEFR1000-A2-0226 (source index 205)

- **Selected sense:** countable noun, a customer's request for goods or services.
- **CEFR evidence:** Cambridge labels this request noun sense **A2**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/order
- **Level finding:** A2 matches the selected noun sense. Keep it separate from instruction, arrangement, sequence and verb senses.
- **Myanmar:** \`မှာယူမှု\` is concise and natural.
- **Existing example:** “The customer placed an order for ten boxes.” Grammatically correct and sense-aligned.
- **Optional original alternative:** “We placed an order for twenty office chairs.”

### 2. service — CEFR1000-A2-0227 (source index 206)

- **Selected example sense:** a system that supplies something people need (the hotel's shuttle service).
- **CEFR evidence:** Cambridge Learner's Dictionary labels the customer-help sense and the service-system sense **B1**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/service
- **Level finding:** the example “The hotel provides a free shuttle service” illustrates the B1 system sense, not an A2 sense. The current definition (“Work done to help customers or other people”) also aligns more closely with Cambridge's B1 customer-service sense. This is a direct A2-versus-B1 conflict.
- **Myanmar:** \`ဝန်ဆောင်မှု\` is natural for the general noun; for the example's transport-system sense, \`ကြိုပို့ယာဉ်ဝန်ဆောင်မှု\` or \`ကြိုပို့ယာဉ်ပြေးဆွဲရေး\` is more precise.
- **Original example proposal if retained/re-levelled:** “The hotel provides a free shuttle service for guests.”

### 3. season — CEFR1000-A2-0228 (source index 207)

- **Selected sense:** noun, a period of the year associated with a particular type of weather.
- **CEFR evidence:** Cambridge labels the four-part year sense and recurring annual-period sense **B1**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/season . Cambridge Essential English Dictionary also labels both senses B1: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/essential-british-english/season
- **Level finding:** the selected weather/annual-period sense is B1 in both inspected Cambridge entries, conflicting with the shortlist's A2 assignment. The rainy-season example is sense-aligned but does not justify lowering the CEFR level.
- **Myanmar:** \`ရာသီ\` is accurate.
- **Existing example:** “The rainy season starts in June.” Natural and aligned with the annual-period sense.

### 4. area — CEFR1000-A2-0229 (source index 208)

- **Selected definition sense:** a region or part of a country/city.
- **CEFR evidence:** Cambridge Learner's Dictionary labels a region of a country or city **A2**, but a part of a building or land set aside for a particular purpose **B1**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/area
- **Level finding:** A2 is supported if the entry is restricted to the region/place sense. The existing example “This area is reserved for visitors” illustrates the B1 designated-purpose sense. This is a definition/example sense mismatch, not a reason to approve the current entry.
- **Definition refinement:** “A region or part of a country, city, or other place.”
- **Myanmar:** \`ဒေသ၊ နယ်မြေ\` is suitable for the A2 region sense.
- **Original A2 example proposal:** “The factory is in an industrial area.”

### 5. business — CEFR1000-A2-0230 (source index 284)

- **Selected example sense:** a company or organisation that sells goods or services.
- **CEFR evidence:** Cambridge Essential English Dictionary distinguishes the activity of buying and selling goods/services (**A1**) from an organisation that sells goods/services (**A2**): https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/essential-british-english/business
- **Level finding:** the shortlist definition (“Work involving buying, selling, or providing goods and services”) describes the A1 activity sense, while the example (“Her family runs a small business near the market”) describes the A2 organisation sense. The A2 placement is supportable only if the definition is corrected to match the example.
- **Definition refinement for A2:** “A company or organisation that sells goods or services.”
- **Myanmar refinement:** \`စီးပွားရေးလုပ်ငန်း၊ ကုမ္ပဏီ\` fits the organisation sense; reserve \`စီးပွားရေးလုပ်ကိုင်ခြင်း၊ ကုန်သွယ်မှု\` for the activity sense.
- **Existing example:** Natural and clearly illustrates the organisation sense.

### 6. however — CEFR1000-A2-0231 (source index 285)

- **Selected sense:** adverb used to introduce a contrast with the preceding statement.
- **CEFR evidence:** Cambridge Learner's Dictionary labels the comparing/contrast use **A2**, while the “no matter how” degree use is **B2**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/however
- **Level finding:** the A2 assignment is supportable for the contrastive connective in the shortlist example. Do not merge it with the B2 degree use or the separate “however did you...?” question use.
- **Myanmar:** \`သို့သော်၊ သို့ရာတွင်\` is natural; the register is more formal than \`ဒါပေမဲ့\`.
- **Existing example:** “The room was small; however, it was comfortable.” Correct semicolon use and clear contrast.

### 7. actually — CEFR1000-A2-0233 (source index 287)

- **Selected example sense:** adverb used to state what is really true.
- **CEFR evidence:** Cambridge Learner's Dictionary labels the truth use **A2**, the surprising/opposite-of-expectation use **B1**, and the disagreement/refusal use **B2**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/actually
- **Level finding:** the example about Tuesday and Wednesday illustrates the A2 truth/correction use. The current definition (“especially when it differs from what someone expected”) risks blending in the B1 surprise sense. Keep the entry's A2 meaning narrow.
- **Definition refinement:** “Used to say what is really true.”
- **Myanmar:** \`တကယ်တော့၊ အမှန်မှာ\` is natural for the truth use.
- **Existing example:** Grammatically correct and suitable for A2 when read as a factual correction.

### 8. less — CEFR1000-A2-0234 (source index 288)

- **Selected sense:** quantifier/determiner for a smaller amount.
- **CEFR evidence:** Cambridge Learner's Dictionary labels the quantifier use **A2**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/less
- **Grammar evidence:** Cambridge Grammar notes that \`less\` is used with singular uncountable nouns; \`fewer\` is normally used with plural countable nouns: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/uk/grammar/british-grammar/determiners-and-types-of-noun
- **Level finding:** A2 matches the selected quantifier sense. The shortlist's uncountable-noun restriction is useful, but a reviewer should check whether \`determiner\` or the broader Cambridge label \`quantifier\` is the preferred POS convention for the project.
- **Myanmar:** \`ပိုနည်းသော၊ လျော့နည်းသော\` is suitable.
- **Existing example:** “Please add less sugar to my tea.” Correct because \`sugar\` is uncountable in this use.

### 9. party — CEFR1000-A2-0235 (source index 289)

- **Selected sense:** countable noun, a social event where people meet and enjoy themselves.
- **CEFR evidence:** Cambridge Learner's Dictionary labels this event sense **A1**; it labels the political-party sense B1: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/party
- **Level finding:** direct A1-versus-A2 conflict for the selected social-event sense. Do not use the political-party or legal-party senses to justify the current A2 placement. Consider re-level or replacement after independent review.
- **Myanmar:** \`ပါတီပွဲ\` is understood, but \`ဧည့်ခံပွဲ\` or \`ပျော်ပွဲရွှင်ပွဲ\` may be more natural depending on the event.
- **Original A1 example proposal:** “We are having a small party for my sister's birthday.”

### 10. whole — CEFR1000-A2-0236 (source index 291)

- **Selected sense:** adjective, complete or not divided.
- **CEFR evidence:** Cambridge labels this adjective sense **A2**: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/whole
- **Level finding:** A2 matches the selected adjective sense. Keep it separate from \`whole\` as a noun meaning an entire amount or thing.
- **Myanmar:** \`တစ်ခုလုံးသော၊ အပြည့်အစုံဖြစ်သော\` is broadly accurate; natural phrasing depends on the noun.
- **Existing example:** “We spent the whole afternoon cleaning the classroom.” Natural and sense-aligned.
