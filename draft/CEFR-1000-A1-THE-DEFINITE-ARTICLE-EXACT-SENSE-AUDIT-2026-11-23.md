## Observed primary references (2026-11-23)
1. Cambridge Learner's Dictionary, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/the — `ALREADY KNOWN` determiner **A1**; `ONLY ONE` determiner **A1**. Different constructions include `COMPARE` **B2** and `BODY` **B1**. A blanket CEFR label for every use of *the* would therefore be inaccurate.
2. Cambridge English Grammar Today, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/a-and-the — distinguishes shared/identifiable reference from introducing an unspecified referent; documents zero article with plural and mass nouns in generic reference, and that *the* is not combined with possessive/demonstrative determiners.
3. British Council LearnEnglish, https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/specific-general-determiners — beginner-level explanation of definite vs indefinite determiners; **not an independently assigned dictionary CEFR level for this exact sense**.

## Editorial findings and candidate repairs (NOT applied)
- Current English is too broad: *particular* alone does not explain that the listener/reader must be able to identify the referent, or that a uniquely identifiable thing may not have been mentioned before.
- Candidate A1 **exact sense**: “Used before a noun when the listener or reader can identify the person or thing, because it is already known or clear from the situation.” Consider a separately explained uniquely existing referent rather than blending higher-level constructions.
- Candidate fluent Myanmar explanation: “ပြောသူနဲ့ နားထောင်သူ နှစ်ဦးစလုံး ဘယ်သူ၊ ဘယ်အရာကို ဆိုလိုမှန်း သိနိုင်တဲ့အခါ နာမ်ရှေ့မှာ သုံးတဲ့ စကားလုံး။” Note that Burmese does not require a word-for-word equivalent article; avoid teaching `the` as always meaning `ဒီ`, `အဲဒီ` or `တစ်ခု`.
- Original paired example 1: “I bought a notebook yesterday. The notebook is blue.” / “မနေ့က မှတ်စုစာအုပ်တစ်အုပ် ဝယ်ခဲ့တယ်။ အဲဒီမှတ်စုစာအုပ်က အပြာရောင်ပါ။” (first mention vs already identifiable).
- Original paired example 2: “Please close the door.” / “တံခါးကို ပိတ်ပေးပါ။” (situationally identifiable door; no obligatory Burmese article).
- Original contrast: “I like music.” / “ကျွန်တော် သီချင်းနားထောင်ရတာ ကြိုက်တယ်။” (generic uncountable noun; do **not** force *the*). This is a teaching contrast, not a replacement entry.
- Existing “The book is on the table.” is grammatical, but without context both definite referents are under-explained. Add a context line or replace with a two-sentence first-mention pair.
- Pronunciation: Cambridge lists UK strong /ðiː/ and weak /ði, ðə/. Require British George/Emma listening in context; do not infer audio completion from dictionary IPA or text synthesis alone.
