## he — male referent, subject position
Cambridge Learner's Dictionary, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/he , marks **A1** for subject pronoun referring to an already discussed male person. Cambridge English Dictionary, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/he , includes male animals and flags generic masculine `he` as old-fashioned and potentially offensive; do not teach it as the default unknown-gender pronoun.

**Original narrow definition proposal:** A subject pronoun for a man or boy already known from the conversation.

**Myanmar draft:** အရင်က ပြောထားပြီးသား အမျိုးသားတစ်ယောက် ဒါမှမဟုတ် ယောက်ျားလေးတစ်ယောက်ကို ပြန်ညွှန်းတဲ့အခါ ဝါကျရဲ့ ကတ္တားနေရာမှာ သုံးတဲ့ နာမ်စား။ မြန်မာလို အများအားဖြင့် “သူ” လို့ ပြောတယ်။

**Original example:** “My uncle is a teacher. He works at a school.”

**Myanmar example:** “ကျွန်တော့်ဦးလေးက ဆရာတစ်ယောက်ပါ။ သူက ကျောင်းမှာ အလုပ်လုပ်တယ်။” (Speaker-register variant `ကျွန်မ့ဦးလေး`; independent native-fluent review required.)

## she — female referent, subject position
Cambridge Learner's Dictionary, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/she , marks **A1** for a subject pronoun referring to an already discussed female person. Cambridge English Dictionary, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/she , includes female animals, but also lists an old-fashioned figurative use for ships/countries; exclude that latter use from this A1 record.

**Original narrow definition proposal:** A subject pronoun for a woman or girl already known from the conversation.

**Myanmar draft:** အရင်က ပြောထားပြီးသား အမျိုးသမီးတစ်ယောက် ဒါမှမဟုတ် မိန်းကလေးတစ်ယောက်ကို ပြန်ညွှန်းတဲ့အခါ ဝါကျရဲ့ ကတ္တားနေရာမှာ သုံးတဲ့ နာမ်စား။ မြန်မာလို “သူ” လို့ပဲ ပြောလေ့ရှိတယ်။

**Original example:** “My aunt lives nearby. She visits us on Sundays.”

**Myanmar example:** “ကျွန်တော့်အဒေါ်က အနီးအနားမှာ နေတယ်။ သူက တနင်္ဂနွေနေ့တွေမှာ ကျွန်တော်တို့ဆီ လာလည်တယ်။” (Speaker and family-term register need independent review.)

## it — previously identified thing, not every impersonal use
Cambridge Learner's Dictionary, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/it , marks the previously discussed **thing/situation/idea** sense **A1**, and marks time/weather use **A2**. Cambridge English Dictionary, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/it , also labels the previously mentioned thing/animal/situation sense A1. The provisional example “It is a small cat” does **not** establish an antecedent, so it is weak evidence for the specified anaphoric sense. The provisional definition omits situations and ideas.

**Original narrow definition proposal:** A pronoun used instead of the name of a thing already mentioned.

**Myanmar draft:** အရင်က ပြောထားပြီးသား ပစ္စည်းတစ်ခုကို ထပ်မပြောချင်တဲ့အခါ အစားထိုးသုံးတဲ့ နာမ်စား။ မြန်မာလို အခြေအနေအလိုက် “အဲဒါ”၊ “အဲဒီဟာ” လို့ ပြောနိုင်ပြီး တချို့ဝါကျတွေမှာ နာမ်စားကို ချန်ထားတာ ပိုသဘာဝကျတယ်။

**Original example:** “Where is my blue bag? It is under the table.”

**Myanmar example:** “ကျွန်တော့်အပြာရောင်အိတ် ဘယ်မှာလဲ။ စားပွဲအောက်မှာ ရှိတယ်။” (Myanmar naturally omits the repeated subject here; reviewer should verify that this is pedagogically clear.)

**Explicit level contrast:** “It is raining.” is an impersonal weather construction, **A2** per Cambridge; do not use it as evidence for the A1 anaphoric `it` sense.

## Editorial risk and reviewer instructions
1. English `he` and `she` express gender distinctions that ordinary Burmese `သူ` generally does not; do not invent different obligatory Burmese pronouns. Do not teach gender-neutral `he` as modern default.
2. `he`/`she` are subject forms; `him`/`her` are object forms. Do not certify the object sense using these subject examples.
3. `it` may be a subject or object, but weather/time `it` is a separate A2 use. Verify the *specific* illustrated sense, not merely the headword.
4. Each proposed Myanmar definition and example needs genuinely independent native-fluent assessment for grammar, register and naturalness; each original English example needs independent grammar/sense review. Source URLs need re-check by reviewer. Neither a dictionary nor this file constitutes reviewer identity/signoff.
