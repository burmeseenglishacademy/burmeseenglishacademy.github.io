## Editorial findings and proposed candidate (not accepted)
- The provisional example “I want to be a teacher” is grammatical but tests **infinitival be after want to**, not the most transparent A1 present-tense copular form. It does not demonstrate `am/is/are` directly.
- Suggested exact sense: **linking verb identifying a person or describing a state**; do not silently conflate existence, identity, location, auxiliary progressive/passive, or future `be going to` as one verified CEFR sense.
- Draft English: “Used to say who or what someone or something is, or what they are like.”
- Draft Myanmar: “လူတစ်ယောက် (သို့) အရာဝတ္ထုတစ်ခုက ဘယ်သူ၊ ဘာဖြစ်တယ်၊ ဘယ်လိုအခြေအနေရှိတယ်ဆိုတာ ဖော်ပြရာမှာ သုံးတဲ့ ကြိယာ။ ပစ္စုပ္ပန်ကာလမှာ am, is, are ဆိုတဲ့ ပုံစံတွေ သုံးတယ်။”
- Original example: “My sister is a nurse.” / “ကျွန်တော့်အစ်မက သူနာပြုတစ်ယောက်ပါ။” Note: speaker-specific `ကျွန်တော့်` is not universally appropriate; a gender-neutral alternative is “အစ်မက သူနာပြုတစ်ယောက်ပါ။” Prefer the latter unless context requires possession.
- Contrast example (original): “They are tired.” / “သူတို့ ပင်ပန်းနေကြတယ်။” Myanmar naturally omits a literal equivalent of the copula here.
- Disambiguation: `She is reading` uses auxiliary `is`; `There is a chair` introduces existence; neither automatically establishes this candidate's exact sense/level.
