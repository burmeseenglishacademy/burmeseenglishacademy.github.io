## Evidence observed 2026-11-20

1. Cambridge English Dictionary, **need**, verb **MUST HAVE**, explicitly **A1**; it also separately labels a beneficial/should-have sense **B1**, and the noun **need** as **B2**. URL: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/need
2. Cambridge English Dictionary, **require**, transitive verb “to need something or make something necessary,” explicitly **B1**. URL: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/require
3. Cambridge Learner's Dictionary distinguishes the general need/demand sense of **require** at **B1** from the construction **require somebody to do something** in the official-demand sense at **B2**. URL: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/require

The observed labels support the *general* sense-level A1/B1 distinction in the current shortlist; they do not prove independent review, or establish that every use of `require` is B1. The existing B1 example describes a job condition and appears consistent with the general necessity sense; an independent reviewer must check whether the intended reading is general necessity or formal obligation.

## Proposed editorial refinements — not applied

- A1 **need**: “To have to have something because it is necessary.” Proposed Myanmar: **မရှိမဖြစ် လိုအပ်သည်** where necessity must be emphasized; **လိုအပ်သည်** remains natural in the existing simple example.
- B1 **require**: “To need something or make something necessary, often as a condition.” Proposed Myanmar in the current employment example: **လိုအပ်သည် / လုပ်ဆောင်ရန် လိုအပ်နိုင်သည်**; avoid automatically translating every `require` as **တောင်းဆိုသည်** or **အမိန့်ပေးသည်**.
- Proposed alternative original B1 example: “The course requires basic computer skills.” Proposed Myanmar: **ဒီသင်တန်းတက်ဖို့ အခြေခံကွန်ပျူတာကျွမ်းကျင်မှု လိုအပ်တယ်။** These are candidate edits only; native Myanmar review and exact-sense CEFR verification remain necessary.
