### 1. manufacturer — C1 entry, Cambridge B2 for corresponding sense

- Review task: `CEFR1000-C1-0867`; source index **457**; POS noun.
- Original English: “A company or person that makes goods, usually in large quantities.”
- Original Myanmar: “ကုန်ပစ္စည်းထုတ်လုပ်သူ၊ ထုတ်လုပ်ရေးကုမ္ပဏီ”
- Original example: “The manufacturer recalled the product after discovering a defect.”
- Cambridge Advanced Learner's Dictionary: **B2** countable noun, a company producing goods in large numbers. Cambridge Learner's Dictionary also gives **B2** for a company making something. These match the example's company sense, not a demonstrably C1-only sense.
- Sources observed 2026-11-21: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/manufacturer ; https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/manufacturer
- Grammar: example is well formed; subject `manufacturer` is an organisation, `recalled` transitive past tense, `after discovering` has understood same agent.
- Myanmar: existing wording communicates both person and company, but the cited B2 sense and example are specifically company; independent Myanmar reviewer should consider a company-specific wording, e.g. “ကုန်ပစ္စည်းထုတ်လုပ်သည့် ကုမ္ပဏီ” (candidate only). Do not treat a company/person distinction as proven C1.
- Disposition: **C1 level unsupported for this documented sense**; request independent sense-specific decision and replacement candidate if C1 target must remain 200.

### 2. fluid — C1 entry, Cambridge C2 for corresponding noun sense

- Review task: `CEFR1000-C1-0853`; source index **410**; POS noun.
- Original English: “A substance that can flow, such as a liquid or gas.”
- Original Myanmar: “စီးဆင်းနိုင်သော အရည် သို့မဟုတ် ဓာတ်ငွေ့”
- Original example: “The mechanic checked the brake fluid.”
- Cambridge Advanced Learner's Dictionary labels **C2** the noun meaning a substance that flows and is not solid, including `power steering fluid`, a close automotive collocation. Cambridge Learner's Dictionary offers a simpler `a liquid` definition without a visible CEFR tag on that page; do not infer a level from the unlabelled entry.
- Sources observed 2026-11-21: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/fluid ; https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/learner-english/fluid
- Grammar: example is grammatical, and `brake fluid` is a compound noun. It instantiates a specific liquid, while the definition additionally covers gases. This is not necessarily an error but deserves exact-sense alignment.
- Myanmar: existing wording correctly captures the broad physical-science category; the automotive example refers specifically to brake hydraulic liquid. Candidate context-specific gloss: “ဘရိတ်ဆီ” for `brake fluid`, **not** a replacement definition of `fluid`. Independent Myanmar review required.
- Disposition: **C1 level not substantiated by the observed Cambridge C2 noun label**; investigate alternative credible CEFR evidence for this exact noun sense rather than silently changing its sense or level.
