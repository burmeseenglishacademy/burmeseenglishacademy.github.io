## Publisher evidence and precise limits
1. Cambridge Learner's Dictionary, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/this?q=this_3 (observed 2026-11-23): labels **A1** for the *determiner NEAR* sense, **A1** for *ALREADY TALKED ABOUT*, and **A1** for *TIME*. The current provisional definition covers only NEAR, not the other A1 uses. Cambridge separately lists the pronoun sense; do not infer that every pronoun usage is A1.
2. Cambridge Grammar Today, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/determiners-the-my-some-this. (observed 2026-11-23): contrasts demonstrative determiners `this`/`that`, explains their noun-phrase role, and contrasts near and farther reference. This is grammatical evidence, **not** a publisher CEFR level judgment.
3. Cambridge Dictionary, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/determiner (observed 2026-11-23): explains that `this`/`that` combine with singular countable or uncountable nouns; `these`/`those` take plural countable nouns. This supports a usage check, not CEFR approval.

## Editorial findings and original candidate wording
- The existing English and Myanmar definitions are compatible with the *near* determiner sense but are too narrow to describe all A1 determiner uses. Do **not** silently expand the approved sense without deciding whether the project is one sense per headword or allows multiple sense records.
- Candidate English for the existing NEAR sense: "Used before a noun to identify someone or something close to the speaker or being pointed at."
- Candidate Myanmar: "ပြောသူအနီးရှိ သို့မဟုတ် လက်ညှိုးထိုးညွှန်ပြနေသော လူ၊ အရာဝတ္ထုကို ရည်ညွှန်းရာတွင် နာမ်ရှေ့၌ သုံးသော 'ဒီ၊ ဒီ...၊ ဤ'။" The standalone `ဒီ` should not imply that `this` is a pronoun in this record; contextual phrasing matters.
- Original near-sense example: "This chair is next to my desk." / "ဒီကုလားထိုင်က ကျွန်တော့်စားပွဲဘေးမှာ ရှိတယ်။"
