## External evidence observed on 2026-11-23
1. Cambridge Learner's Dictionary, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/learner-english/you — `PERSON SPOKEN TO` is explicitly **A1**, while `PEOPLE GENERALLY` is separately **A2**. These are distinct lexical uses of the same spelling.
2. Cambridge English Dictionary, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/you — `PERSON/PEOPLE ADDRESSED` **A1**, `PEOPLE GENERALLY` **A2**; British English pronunciation variants shown. Publisher label applies to the specified sense, not all possible uses of `you`.
3. Cambridge Grammar, https://dictionary.cambridge.org/uk/grammar/british-grammar/pronouns-personal-i-me-you-he-it-etc — `you` is both singular and plural, and has identical subject/object forms. Grammar corroboration, not itself CEFR evidence.
4. British Council LearnEnglish, https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/personal-pronouns — subject/object paradigms confirm `you` in both roles and describe generic `you`; grammar corroboration, not a separate exact-sense CEFR label.

## Editorial assessment (not independently reviewed)
- **A1 sense support:** The shortlist definition and original example clearly indicate direct address, matching Cambridge's A1 addressed-person sense. Retain A1 as a *candidate*, pending independent publisher/source and bilingual review.
- **Sense boundary:** Do not use a generic-person example such as “You never know what will happen” as interchangeable evidence for this A1 entry: Cambridge assigns generic `you` A2.
- **Myanmar register risk:** `သင်` is often formal/written, `မင်း` familiar and potentially inappropriate toward elders, and `ရှင်` context-/speaker-dependent. The existing comma-separated gloss does not express plural addressees or social register. Exact register and inclusive choices need an independent fluent Myanmar reviewer; do not imply these are universally interchangeable.
- **Candidate English definition:** “The person or people being spoken or written to.” This also covers direct written address without drifting into generic `you`.
- **Candidate Myanmar explanation:** “စကားပြောနေသူက မိမိပြောဆိုဆက်သွယ်နေသည့် တစ်ဦး သို့မဟုတ် လူအများကို ရည်ညွှန်းသည့် နာမ်စား။ မြန်မာဘာသာပြန်ရာတွင် အသက်အရွယ်၊ ရင်းနှီးမှုနှင့် ယဉ်ကျေးမှုအလိုက် အသုံးအနှုန်းကို ရွေးရသည်။” This is a descriptive explanation, not a universally valid one-word translation.
- **Original candidate example (singular):** “You can sit here, May.” Suggested Myanmar: “မေ၊ ဒီမှာ ထိုင်လို့ရတယ်။”
- **Original candidate example (plural):** “You can all sit here.” Suggested Myanmar: “အားလုံး ဒီမှာ ထိုင်လို့ရပါတယ်။”
- **Example QA nuance:** Natural Myanmar may omit an explicit second-person pronoun where English requires `you`. That is acceptable in translation if the addressed-person meaning remains unambiguous; do not force awkward literal pronouns.
- **Audio:** Neither British George nor Emma recordings, nor their actual listening signoff, are evidenced by this investigation. Subject/object unstressed realizations require separate audio QA.
