## First-pass exact-sense findings
- Existing `a` definition: “Used before one person or thing when it is not specified.” Accurate in broad outline but **underspecified**: singular **countable noun**, first mention/non-identifiable referent, and consonant **sound** (not consonant letter) are relevant. Current example `I have a book.` is grammatical and illustrates indefinite singular count noun. It does not demonstrate sound-vs-letter exceptions.
- Existing `an` definition: “Used before one person or thing when the next word starts with a vowel sound.” The vowel-sound criterion is correct; add singular count noun and indefiniteness to align with `a`. Current example `She has an apple.` is grammatical and appropriate.
- These two spellings are **allomorphs of the same indefinite article**, not unrelated lexical meanings. The manifest should decide explicitly whether two separate learning cards are pedagogically justified and record that policy, rather than silently treating them as two unrelated senses. Preserve the 200-per-level target; any merge or replacement requires editorial control and fresh independent review.
- Avoid explaining `a/an` as always meaning Myanmar numeral `တစ်`: Burmese does not generally require an English-style article. Literal numeral translations can falsely imply numerical emphasis.
